## 1. ArcGIS CityEngine and CGA shape grammar

### Takeaway
CGA is a text grammar whose unit of state is a shape with an oriented bounding box (the scope) and a pivot; rules refine shapes by component split (`comp`) and subdivision (`split`, with absolute, relative and floating sizes plus a repeat operator), and terminal assets are inserted with `i()`, which by default stretches the asset's bounding box to coincide with the scope. The asset itself needs no markup at all (no sockets, tags or declared front): everything it "is" comes from the rule and scope that places it; the only asset-side levers are its bounding box, its up axis and its own origin (used only when size is kept).

### Cited Findings
- `i()` signatures: `i(filePath)`, `i(filePath, upAxisOfGeometry)`, `i(filePath, upAxisOfGeometry, insertMode)`, `i(filePath, upAxisOfGeometry, insertMode, cleanupMode)` — [Esri CGA reference: i operation](https://doc.arcgis.com/en/cityengine/latest/cga/cga-i.htm)
- `upAxisOfGeometry` is `yUp` (default) or `zUp` (geometry rotated -90 degrees about x to match CityEngine's coordinate system) — [Esri CGA: i](https://doc.arcgis.com/en/cityengine/latest/cga/cga-i.htm)
- `insertMode` values: `alignSizeAndPosition` (default; "The geometry is fit to the scope"), `keepSizeAndPosition` (size and position kept, equivalent to `s(0,0,0)`), `keepSizeAlignPosition` (size kept; geometry centred on the scope's xz-plane with its bounding box resting on y=0) — [Esri CGA: i](https://doc.arcgis.com/en/cityengine/latest/cga/cga-i.htm)
- Default fitting: "The asset is transformed such that its bounding box coincides with the scope." That is non-uniform stretch on all three axes — [Esri CGA: i](https://doc.arcgis.com/en/cityengine/latest/cga/cga-i.htm)
- Zero scope sizes select other fitting behaviours: all three zero means the scope adopts the asset's size and translation (original dimensions preserved); two zero means uniform scale driven by the one non-zero dimension (proportions preserved); one zero means that dimension is set from the average scale of the other two — [Esri CGA: i](https://doc.arcgis.com/en/cityengine/latest/cga/cga-i.htm)
- If the shape carries trim planes from a component split, the inserted model is cut by them; the `trim` attribute toggles this and `geometry.isInstanced` reports whether an inserted asset remained an untrimmed instance — [Esri CGA: i](https://doc.arcgis.com/en/cityengine/latest/cga/cga-i.htm)
- Accepted asset formats: OBJ, COLLADA DAE, FBX, glTF 2.0, USD (UsdGeomMesh only), DWG (3D elements only), IFC 2x3. FBX custom attributes are ignored — [Esri CGA: i](https://doc.arcgis.com/en/cityengine/latest/cga/cga-i.htm)
- Asset constraint stated by Esri: "All faces of a mesh must have the same usage of vertices, texture coordinates and vertex normals"; otherwise UVs or normals are dropped — [Esri CGA: i](https://doc.arcgis.com/en/cityengine/latest/cga/cga-i.htm)
- `comp(component) { selector operator operations | ... }`; component types f (faces), e (edges), fe, v, fv, g (groups), m (materials), h (holes) — [Esri CGA: comp](https://doc.arcgis.com/en/cityengine/latest/cga/cga-comp.htm)
- Front is computed, not declared. Scope-relative selectors (front, back, left, right, top, bottom): "The y-normals of the components are analyzed by classifying their directions into the corresponding quadrants (in relation to the scope coordinate system of the current shape)." There are also object-relative (`object.front` ...), world-relative (`world.north` ...), and street-relative (`street.front`, `street.back`, `street.side` ...) selectors; street selectors depend on the `streetWidth` attribute and fall back to `object.*` if it is absent — [Esri CGA: comp](https://doc.arcgis.com/en/cityengine/latest/cga/cga-comp.htm)
- After `comp(f)`, each face shape gets a new local frame: "the x-axis will be parallel to the first edge of the face and the z-axis wil point along the face's normal"; "The pivot will be positioned at the first vertex of the first edge of the face"; the scope is the face's bounding box with z-size zero — [Esri CGA: comp](https://doc.arcgis.com/en/cityengine/latest/cga/cga-comp.htm)
- `comp` operators: `:` one shape per component, `=` all selected components as one shape, `=:` one shape per connected set. Selection is exclusive left to right. Trim planes are generated for faces with `:`; vertical trim planes are on by default, horizontal off (this is the corner-mitre mechanism) — [Esri CGA: comp](https://doc.arcgis.com/en/cityengine/latest/cga/cga-comp.htm)
- `split(axis) { size : operations | ... }` with three size kinds: absolute (no prefix, exact size), relative (`'`, fraction of scope), floating (`~`): "the remaining spaces between the split parts with absolute dimensions are automatically adapted. If multiple floating parts are defined within a split, the dimensions are weighed proportionally." — [Esri CGA: split](https://doc.arcgis.com/en/cityengine/latest/cga/cga-split.htm)
- Repeat `*`: "the content of {...} is repeated as often as it fully fits into the scope's dimension along the selected axis." With absolute sizes the last repetition is cut off; with floating sizes "the sizes are adjusted such that the ratio between the elements is kept and the whole scope can be filled" — [Esri CGA: split](https://doc.arcgis.com/en/cityengine/latest/cga/cga-split.htm)
- Rhythm patterns come from nesting, e.g. `{ { 1 : X(3) | ~3 : Y(1) }* | 1 : X(3) }` gives an ABAB...A pattern; nesting depth is in theory unlimited — [Esri CGA: split](https://doc.arcgis.com/en/cityengine/latest/cga/cga-split.htm)
- Origin of the grammar: Müller, Wonka, Haegler, Ulmer, Van Gool, "Procedural Modeling of Buildings", ACM TOG 25(3), 2006, pp. 614-623, DOI 10.1145/1141911.1141931; it introduces CGA shape, defines split including the repeat split and scaling of rules, and introduces the component split "the basis for modeling with one-, two-, and three-dimensional shapes" **[search summary of the paper's abstract/secondary descriptions; full text not fetched]** — [DOI via Unpaywall](https://unpaywall.org/10.1145%2F1141911.1141931), [secondary summary, gamedev.net](https://gamedev.net/tutorials/programming/engines-and-middleware/procedural-modeling-of-buildings-with-shape-grammars-r4596)

### Inferences
- The CGA asset contract is effectively "a mesh with a meaningful axis-aligned bounding box in a known up-axis convention". Because default insertion maps bbox to scope, the asset's own pivot is irrelevant in the default mode and only matters for `keepSizeAndPosition`. An asset meant to be stretched must tolerate non-uniform scale; one that must not stretch is handled rule-side (zero a scope dimension, or use `keepSize...`), not by an asset flag.
- "Stretch versus repeat" is entirely a rule decision in CGA: `~size` with `*` gives "repeat an integer number of times and stretch each slightly to close the gap", which is the canonical answer to variable-length walls.
- Corners are handled by trim planes (mitre cut of geometry that overshoots a face) rather than by dedicated corner assets, though a rule can also split off a corner strip and insert a corner asset.

### Gaps
- Full text of Müller et al. 2006 could not be fetched (course mirror returned 404). Details such as the paper's scope notation (P, X/Y/Z, S), `Subdiv`/`Repeat`/`Comp` notation, the `I("id")` insert instruction, occlusion queries and snap lines are **[unverified, background]** in this pass.
- Parish and Müller 2001 "Procedural Modeling of Cities" (L-system street growth, lot subdivision) was not researched in this pass; no cited findings.
- How CityEngine preserves hand edits across regeneration was not verified. **[unverified, background]**: CityEngine exposes rule attributes per shape (overridable in the Inspector), handles, and a "Local Edits" feature that stores per-split-node attribute overrides; these need confirmation in Esri docs before use.
- The CGA extrusion and setback operations (`extrude`, `setback`, `offset`, roof operations) were not fetched; the lot-to-mass-model step is described only via the paper's secondary summaries.

## 2. Houdini: SideFX Labs Building Generator, Houdini Engine instancing, city pipelines

### Takeaway
The Labs Building Generator takes a low-resolution blockout volume, slices it into floors, classifies regions (wall, corner, ledge) and replaces them with modules looked up by name pattern from a user library; leftover width is absorbed by a dedicated filler ("sideslop") module rather than by declared sockets. Houdini Engine then hands instances to Unreal through point or packed-primitive attributes such as `unreal_instance`. Official docs say little about required module dimensions or pivots.

### Cited Findings
- Input and process: the node takes "low-resolution blockout meshes", "analyzes incoming building volumes", slices into floors, and "identifies structural regions such as walls, corners, and ledges, and replaces those regions with high-resolution modules sourced from a user-defined module library" — [SideFX docs: Labs Building Generator 4.0](https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/nodes/sop/labs--building_generator-4.0.html)
- Modules are referenced by name: Facade Module Pattern is the "Module name used as the primary wall unit"; there are separate parameters for Concave Corner Module, Convex Corner Module, top and bottom ledge (each with height and a Module Pattern; "Simple Ledges" substitutes extrusions for ledge modules) — [SideFX docs: Labs Building Generator 4.0](https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/nodes/sop/labs--building_generator-4.0.html)
- Variable wall length: a Sideslop module is the "Module used to fill the gap between primary wall modules and corner modules" — [SideFX docs: Labs Building Generator 4.0](https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/nodes/sop/labs--building_generator-4.0.html)
- Floors: "Full Floor Height" sets a single floor's height; Floor Overrides are per floor (Floor Index, 0 = ground; Floor Height); a Seed controls randomisation — [SideFX docs: Labs Building Generator 4.0](https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/nodes/sop/labs--building_generator-4.0.html)
- A companion node, Labs Building Generator Utility, "Creates and configures building modules for use with the Labs Building Generator" — [SideFX docs: Labs Building Generator 4.0](https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/nodes/sop/labs--building_generator-4.0.html)
- Community naming convention **[search summary of SideFX forum posts, may be outdated]**: modules are named `BaseName*Variation` (for example `Wall*A`, `Wall*B`), and the Facade Module Pattern `Wall*` picks up all variants; users report writing custom VEX inside the node to distinguish concave from convex corners — [SideFX forum thread](https://www.sidefx.com/forum/topic/74033/), [forum post](https://www.sidefx.com/forum/post/373804/)
- Houdini Engine for Unreal: "The plug-in supports outputting instances from an asset using packed primitives, object instancers" and attribute instancers; "The recommended way to create instances is using Packed primitives." — [SideFX: Houdini Engine for Unreal, Instancing](https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/unreal/instancing.html)
- Attribute instancers reference an existing Unreal asset (or a class name such as `PointLight`) through `unreal_instance` as a point or detail attribute; "attribute instancers need to be in a separate part of the Geometry." Transform comes from the point plus "the Houdini rot/orient and scale attributes" — [SideFX: Instancing](https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/unreal/instancing.html)
- Related attributes: `unreal_split_attr` (one component per attribute value; `unreal_split_instances` is deprecated), `unreal_hierarchical_instancer`, `unreal_force_instancer`, `unreal_foliage`, `unreal_num_custom_floats` and `unreal_per_instance_custom_dataX` for per-instance custom data — [SideFX: Instancing](https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/unreal/instancing.html)

### Inferences
- The Labs Building Generator's asset contract is a name plus a role assigned by which parameter the name is typed into (wall, convex corner, concave corner, ledge, sideslop). Role is a property of the placement slot, not something the mesh declares, except through its name.
- The doc's wording maps "Concave Corner Module" to outward-facing corners and "Convex" to inward-facing, which is the reverse of common usage; treat the labels as unreliable and test.
- The Houdini-to-engine hand-off shows a minimal, engine-agnostic placement record: (asset reference string, position, orientation, scale, optional per-instance floats, optional grouping key).

### Gaps
- Exact module dimension, pivot and facing requirements for the Labs Building Generator were not found in official docs in this pass (the Utility node's doc URL guessed returned 404, and the main page beyond the first 100k characters was not read). **[unverified, background]**: the Utility node writes module attributes (name, width/height, pivot style) and older versions expected modules authored facing a fixed axis with a bottom-corner or bottom-centre pivot.
- Whether the generator supports per-face or painted-attribute overrides on the blockout volume (which would be its hand-edit mechanism) was not confirmed; only per-floor overrides were confirmed.
- No published city pipeline built on the Labs Building Generator was verified in this pass. **[unverified, background]**: Epic's "The Matrix Awakens" / City Sample used a Houdini pipeline exporting point clouds consumed by Unreal rule processors; SideFX published "Project Titan" as a Houdini plus Unreal PCG showcase. Both need sourcing.

## 3. Unreal Engine PCG framework and PCG shape grammar

### Takeaway
PCG operates on points carrying a transform, a bounds box, density, steepness, colour, seed and arbitrary metadata attributes; meshes are spawned from points by the Static Mesh Spawner, optionally selected by an attribute. The grammar feature is a string language over user-defined symbols; a module is (Symbol, Size, Scalable, Debug Color) and the mesh is bound to the symbol separately by attribute matching, so the mesh itself declares nothing: size is read from its bounds and its pivot is corrected in-graph.

### Cited Findings
- A PCG point carries: Transform (translation, rotation, scale); BoundsMin and BoundsMax ("Minimum and maximum extents of the volume represented by the point"); Color (4 channels); Density (0..1 falloff); Steepness ("Softness of the volume represented by a point"); Seed ("computed from the position in order to be world-position consistent") — [Epic: PCG data types reference](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/procedural-content-generation-framework-data-types-reference-in-unreal-engine) (page header UE 5.9 at fetch; version of first appearance not stated)
- Spatial data types: Volumes, Surfaces, Lines (splines and landscape splines), Points, and Polygon 2D; attribute sets are "user-defined variables and stored in the PCG graph as Metadata" — [Epic: PCG data types reference](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/procedural-content-generation-framework-data-types-reference-in-unreal-engine)
- Grammar syntax (as documented under the UE 5.9 header): modules are "User-defined symbols that represent assets. These can be letters or words."; `A, B` is a sequence; `*` repeats as many times as possible; `+` at least once then as many as possible; `[A, B]` groups, with a count as in `[A, B]2`; `{A:2, B:1}` is a weighted random choice (default weight 1; "Only modules that still fit in the remaining space are considered"); `<A, B, C>` is a priority choice using the first that fits — [Epic: Using Shape Grammar with PCG](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/using-shape-grammar-with-pcg-in-unreal-engine)
- Module definition: Symbol and Size are required; Scalable and Debug Color are optional. Modules can be defined on the node or supplied by attribute set through "Module Info as Input" — [Epic: Using Shape Grammar with PCG](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/using-shape-grammar-with-pcg-in-unreal-engine)
- Carriers and nodes: Subdivide Spline (modules placed so "both ends fall on the spline"; Module Height default 100; Center Bounds on by default), Subdivide Segment (subdivides along a chosen local axis, default X), Spline to Segment (one point per segment at its midpoint, optionally extracting angles, connectivity and clockwise info), Duplicate Cross-Sections (copies a spline along an extrusion vector, default (0,0,1000), writes Spline Index), Primitive Cross-Section (turns primitives into cross-section splines), Select Grammar (chooses a grammar string per point by comparing a key, default `$ScaledLocalSize.X`, against ordered criteria), Print Grammar (debug) — [Epic: Using Shape Grammar with PCG](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/using-shape-grammar-with-pcg-in-unreal-engine)
- Primitive Cross-Section belongs to the PCG Geometry Script Interop plugin, which is "in Beta and disabled by default" (as of the 5.9-headed page) — [Epic: Using Shape Grammar with PCG](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/using-shape-grammar-with-pcg-in-unreal-engine)
- Output attributes on generated points: Symbol, Size, Scalable, Debug Color; optional Module Index, IsFirst, IsFinal, Extremity Neighbor Index (off by default). "Forward Attributes From Modules Info" copies the module row's own attributes — [Epic: Using Shape Grammar with PCG](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/using-shape-grammar-with-pcg-in-unreal-engine)
- Scalable modules are stretched to fill leftover space; in the fence tutorial the module is marked Scalable "so the last fence section can stretch to fill the spline." — [Epic: Creating a Fence Generator using Shape Grammar](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/creating-a-fence-generator-using-shape-grammar-in-unreal-engine)
- Mesh binding in Epic's tutorial: a Mesh Info array of static meshes; a subgraph derives each module's Size "directly from the selected mesh's bounds" (X extent times two); Match And Set Attributes joins on `Symbol`; Static Mesh Spawner uses `PCGMeshSelectorByAttribute` with attribute `Mesh` — [Epic: Fence Generator tutorial](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/creating-a-fence-generator-using-shape-grammar-in-unreal-engine)
- Pivot assumption: subdivision "places a pivot point in the middle of the bounds"; if the mesh pivot is elsewhere the tutorial computes a PivotOffset from `$LocalCenter`, scaled by `$Scale` and rotated by the point transform — [Epic: Fence Generator tutorial](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/creating-a-fence-generator-using-shape-grammar-in-unreal-engine)
- Example production grammar string from Epic: `{[A,P]:2,[BL,P]:1,[BS,P]:1}*,[G,P], {[A,P]:2,[BL,P]:1,[BS,P]:1}*` (panels with posts, a gate in the middle) — [Epic: Fence Generator tutorial](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/creating-a-fence-generator-using-shape-grammar-in-unreal-engine)
- Version: the same doc pages are also linked with `application_version=5.5`, and mention behaviour changes "before 5.6" (a Spline Direction warning for nodes saved before 5.6) — [Epic: Using Shape Grammar with PCG](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/using-shape-grammar-with-pcg-in-unreal-engine)
- Conflict on first version: a third-party transcript of an Epic talk says "the shape grammar nodes we've added in 5.4" **[search summary of a transcript aggregator; not official documentation; unconfirmed]** — [lilys.ai transcript](https://lilys.ai/notes/350556). The task brief and the doc's `application_version=5.5` links point to 5.5. Treat "first shipped in 5.5 (experimental)" as likely but not confirmed here.

### Inferences
- The PCG grammar is one-dimensional: it fills a length (a spline or a segment) with sized symbols. Two-dimensional facades are obtained by composing carriers (Duplicate Cross-Sections for floors, then Subdivide Spline per floor; or Subdivide Segment on a second axis), not by a 2D grammar. This is weaker than CGA's nested splits but has the same primitive: sized module, repeat, stretch flag.
- "What must an asset declare?" Nothing intrinsically. The contract lives in a module table: symbol, length along the subdivision axis, scalable flag, and a mesh reference. The mesh must have bounds that equal its intended footprint along the axis and either a centred pivot or a recorded pivot offset.
- "Scalable" is the only stretch/repeat distinction and is per module, binary. Corners are not a module property; they come from segment extremity attributes (IsFirst/IsFinal, extracted angles) that the graph author uses to choose corner pieces.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm from official docs in which engine version each grammar operator (`+`, `<...>` priority, counts) or node first appeared, nor the feature's maturity label (Experimental/Beta/Production-ready) per version. The 5.5 release notes were not fetched.
- "Create Surface From Spline" and spline-interior carriers were not on the grammar page; not verified as part of the grammar feature.
- How PCG preserves hand edits on regeneration was not verified. **[unverified, background]**: PCG regenerates deterministically from seed and inputs; manual changes are normally expressed as input edits (moving splines, tagged exclusion volumes, difference nodes) rather than edits to generated output, and "Clear PCG Link" detaches output into static actors that no longer regenerate.
- Per-point metadata attributes are described at the graph level only on the fetched page; the statement that arbitrary attributes ride on each point is standard PCG usage but was not quoted from the page.

## 4. Dungeon Architect: markers, themes, snap builders

### Takeaway
Dungeon Architect separates layout from dressing with a three-step pipeline (layout, markers, theme): the builder emits named, oriented markers and a theme graph maps each marker name to candidate meshes with selection, offset and frequency rules. Its snap builders instead stitch hand-built room levels at explicitly placed connection actors whose arrow points outward; each connection asset supplies both a door and a wall-fill mesh, used depending on whether the connection ends up stitched.

### Cited Findings
- Pipeline: generated levels go through layout, markers, theme. The builder marks each spot needing art "with a marker: a name, plus where it is and which way it faces." — [Dungeon Architect docs: How it works](https://docs.dungeonarchitect.dev/unreal/start-here/how-it-works/)
- Example marker vocabulary (Cell Flow builder): `Ground`, `Ceiling`, `Wall`, `WallHalf`, `WallSeparator`, `Door`, `DoorOneWay`, `Stair` — [DA: How it works](https://docs.dungeonarchitect.dev/unreal/start-here/how-it-works/)
- A theme is "a set of rules that says what to place on each marker"; each marker has a list of nodes and "Rules on those nodes decide which one is picked, how it's rotated or offset, and how often it appears." — [DA: How it works](https://docs.dungeonarchitect.dev/unreal/start-here/how-it-works/)
- A Pattern Matcher "looks at the markers around each spot and adds new ones where a pattern matches" (context-sensitive marker rewriting) — [DA: How it works](https://docs.dungeonarchitect.dev/unreal/start-here/how-it-works/)
- Hand-made and generated content share the vocabulary: "A hand drawn blockout room emits the same markers as a procedurally generated one, so the same theme works on both." — [DA: How it works](https://docs.dungeonarchitect.dev/unreal/start-here/how-it-works/)
- Determinism: "The same seed always gives the same level, on every machine."; "Change the theme and every matching spot updates."; the Landscape Transformer "works in its own edit layers, so your landscape is never overwritten" — [DA: How it works](https://docs.dungeonarchitect.dev/unreal/start-here/how-it-works/)
- Builders available: Cell Flow, Grid Flow, Snap Grid Flow, Snap Map, classic Grid, plus a Blockout Designer — [DA: Unreal overview](https://docs.dungeonarchitect.dev/unreal/unreal-overview/)
- Snap Map: "This system lets you stitch together pre-built rooms based on a procedural graph"; "These graphs are generated using Graph Grammar rules" — [DA: Snap Map overview](https://docs.dungeonarchitect.dev/unreal/snap-flow/snapflow-builder-overview/)
- "A module is a pre-designed room", authored in its own level file, with holes left at possible entries and a connection actor placed at each — [DA: Design your modules](https://docs.dungeonarchitect.dev/unreal/snap-flow/snapflow-design-module/)
- Connection contract: place the connection on the door opening and "Make sure the alignment arrow is pointing outwards". The connection asset specifies a Door mesh/Blueprint and a Wall mesh; if another module is stitched through it the Door is placed, otherwise the gap is filled with the Wall mesh. The door mesh should be twice the wall thickness because it is centred on the arrow and covers the adjacent room's wall — [DA: Connections](https://docs.dungeonarchitect.dev/unreal/snap-flow/snapflow-connections/)
- Further Snap Map doc pages exist for Module Database, registering modules, custom snap bound shapes and negation volumes (titles seen in navigation; content not fetched) — [DA: Snap Map overview](https://docs.dungeonarchitect.dev/unreal/snap-flow/snapflow-builder-overview/)

### Inferences
- Marker and theme is the cleanest example of "role is a property of the placement, not the asset": the layout emits (role name, transform with facing), and any asset can be bound to a role with a per-binding offset to correct its pivot. Asset-side requirements reduce to "fits the builder's cell size after the offset".
- The snap connection is a typed, oriented socket in the minimal sense: a frame with outward normal, plus a fallback filler when unused. The "unused socket becomes wall" rule is a compact answer to open connections that a street or building kit also needs (unused door, dead-end road stub).

### Gaps
- Theme graph internals (per-node probability/affinity, "consume on attach", child marker emitters) were not on the fetched pages; **[unverified, background]** that these exist under those names.
- Module database fields (category such as Room/Corridor, allow-rotation, how bounds are computed and collision-checked) not fetched; whether connections are typed beyond one connection asset per kind is unconfirmed. **[unverified, background]**: older versions offered a connection constraint (magnet versus plug/socket polarity).
- The docs do not say whether manual edits to generated actors survive a rebuild; only theme and seed determinism and landscape edit layers are documented on the page read.

## 5. Wave Function Collapse and shipped uses

### Takeaway
WFC replaces generative rules with local adjacency constraints: in the simple tiled model the author supplies a tile list, a symmetry class per tile and allowed neighbour pairs; in the overlapping model adjacency is learned from an example. Shipped games (Bad North, Townscaper) avoid hand-written adjacency lists by deriving compatibility from the tile geometry at shared faces and then add tile-level metadata (navigability, priority) for design control.

### Cited Findings
- Overlapping model: reads an input bitmap, counts NxN patterns (rotations/reflections optional; "In the examples a typical value of N is 3") and requires that "The output should contain only those NxN patterns of pixels that are present in the input." — [mxgmn/WaveFunctionCollapse README](https://github.com/mxgmn/WaveFunctionCollapse)
- Simple tiled model: initialised "with a list of tiles and their adjacency data... rather than a sample bitmap"; "The propagation phase in this model is just adjacency constraint propagation." — [mxgmn/WaveFunctionCollapse](https://github.com/mxgmn/WaveFunctionCollapse)
- Author burden reduction: because adjacency lists get long, "each tile should be assigned with its symmetry type" and adjacent pairs are enumerated only up to symmetry — [mxgmn/WaveFunctionCollapse](https://github.com/mxgmn/WaveFunctionCollapse)
- Edge labels are not always sufficient: the README notes tilesets that are not Wang tiles, where "their adjacency data cannot be induced from edge labels" — [mxgmn/WaveFunctionCollapse](https://github.com/mxgmn/WaveFunctionCollapse)
- Lineage: "Merrell derives adjacency constraints between tiles from an example model and generates a new larger model with the AC-3 algorithm"; WFC generalises this to NxN overlapping patterns — [mxgmn/WaveFunctionCollapse](https://github.com/mxgmn/WaveFunctionCollapse)
- Shipped uses listed by the author: "WFC generates levels in Bad North, Caves of Qud, Dead Static Drive, Townscaper, Matrix Awakens..." — [mxgmn/WaveFunctionCollapse](https://github.com/mxgmn/WaveFunctionCollapse)
- Bad North: over 300 tiles authored in Maya and imported to Unity, including multi-cell tiles; tile edges are scanned to find which other tiles can connect (adjacency derived from geometry, not hand-listed); additional hard constraints plus soft rules (no identical or mirrored neighbours); "each tile that's being placed knows its navigability"; on validation failure the generator resets to a saved post-constraint state — [Game Developer: How Townscaper works](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/how-townscaper-works-a-story-four-games-in-the-making)
- Townscaper: irregular quad grid made by splitting a hex grid into quads, subdividing and relaxing; marching-cubes-style corner-based modules ("corner segments" rather than whole building chunks); WFC runs live on each player edit and re-evaluates the connected structure; "in Townscaper, it is actually allowed to fail silently"; modules have priorities (for example gardens only in enclosed areas next to walls); windows are stencil-cut rather than modelled into tiles; a re-skin would need "somewhere in the region of 500 tiles" — [Game Developer: How Townscaper works](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/how-townscaper-works-a-story-four-games-in-the-making)
- Hand edits in Townscaper: the player only adds/removes blocks and sets colours; everything else is re-derived each edit (a mixed-initiative system) — [Game Developer: How Townscaper works](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/how-townscaper-works-a-story-four-games-in-the-making)
- Townscaper "combined WFC with marching cubes on irregular grids" (Gumin's description) — [mxgmn/WaveFunctionCollapse](https://github.com/mxgmn/WaveFunctionCollapse)

### Inferences
- WFC module markup comes in three strengths: (1) explicit neighbour pair lists plus symmetry class (Gumin's tiled model); (2) per-face socket labels, with compatibility = equal or complementary labels (the common tutorial formulation; not sourced here); (3) no markup: compatibility computed from the mesh cross-section at each face (Bad North). Option 3 moves the contract into modelling discipline: geometry must land exactly on the cell boundary and match vertex-for-vertex.
- In corner-based (marching cubes / dual grid) schemes such as Townscaper, "front" and "corner" stop being asset roles altogether: each module is one of the configurations of solid/empty at a cell's 8 corners, so walls, outer corners and inner corners are just different configurations, and rotation variants are generated, not authored.
- WFC has no notion of a stretchable piece; variable lengths are met only by repetition on the grid, and deformation to an irregular grid (Townscaper) is done by warping the whole module to its cell.

### Gaps
- The exact XML form of Gumin's tileset files (symmetry letters X, I, T, L, \, F; `left`/`right` neighbour entries; per-tile weights) was not on the README text fetched; **[unverified, background]**.
- Stålberg's own talks (for example EPC 2018 on Bad North, SGC 2021 "Beyond Townscaper") were not fetched; the precise geometric matching rule (vertex comparison on module boundaries, treatment of symmetric versus asymmetric faces, vertical versus horizontal faces) is **[unverified, background]**.
- Merrell's model synthesis papers were not fetched directly; lineage is via Gumin's README.
- "Matrix Awakens" use of WFC is asserted by Gumin's README only; not checked against Epic sources.

## 6. Other systems: Blender (Buildify), Tiny Glade, Unity tools, modular samples

### Takeaway
Buildify is the best-documented kit-driven Blender generator found: roles are assigned by putting modules into named collections (and by order inside a collection for corner variants), and walls are stretched to fit. Nothing citable was found in this pass for Tiny Glade's internals, Unity tools or Epic's modular building samples.

### Cited Findings
- Buildify 1.0 (Pavel Oliva) is a free Blender geometry-nodes library that generates buildings from libraries of building parts — [CG Channel](https://www.cgchannel.com/?p=137897), [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/grab-a-free-geometry-nodes-library-for-procedural-buildings-in-blender)
- Buildify structure **[search summary of an unofficial mirror of the docs]**: three main node groups (walls, flat roof, wall props); corner modules are identified by order in a collection: flat pillar first, then the concave-corner pillar, then the convex-corner pillar; an offset parameter exists for kits whose corner pieces sit flush with walls (to avoid z-fighting) — [Buildify documentation mirror (Google Docs)](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1xOR41G5D2-gxJGu3MyR5Oi-2VJvoz7js11W7Cit-G-Q/mobilebasic)
- Buildify variable lengths **[search summary, same mirror]**: it "always tries to stretch the wall modules as best as possible to fit the shape of the building"; the suggested fix when stretching looks bad is to add an edge loop or subdivide the base-mesh edge — [Buildify documentation mirror](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1xOR41G5D2-gxJGu3MyR5Oi-2VJvoz7js11W7Cit-G-Q/mobilebasic)
- Tiny Glade: only press coverage of feature demos was found (procedural windows that merge into groups; procedural tower roofs), by Anastasia Opara of Pounce Light; no technical description of the generation method — [80.lv on windows](https://80.lv/articles/procedural-windows-in-pounce-light-s-tiny-glade-get-an-upgrade/), [80.lv on tower roofs](https://80.lv/articles/a-look-at-procedural-tower-roofs-in-tiny-glade)

### Inferences
- Buildify shows the low-tech end of role declaration: role = collection membership, variant = position in collection, with no per-asset attributes. Its hand-edit model is the base mesh: the user edits a low-poly blockout and the dressing follows, the same pattern as the Houdini Labs generator and Dungeon Architect blockouts.

### Gaps
- Buildify's module size/grid requirements and pivot convention were not found (docs mirror not fetched directly; official source is the author's Gumroad page).
- Tiny Glade: no reliable source found on its internals in this pass. **[unverified, background]**: the developers have said publicly that it does not use WFC and is built from bespoke procedural generators in a custom Rust/Bevy-ECS engine; a talk or interview needs to be located before citing.
- Unity tools (for example Tessera for WFC, BuildR, Archimatix), Epic's modular building samples, Substance and "Citadel" were not researched; no findings.

### Cited Findings
- Front as placement property (CGA): selectors classify face normals relative to scope/object/world/street frames — [Esri CGA: comp](https://doc.arcgis.com/en/cityengine/latest/cga/cga-comp.htm)
- Front as placement property (Dungeon Architect): a marker is "a name, plus where it is and which way it faces" — [DA: How it works](https://docs.dungeonarchitect.dev/unreal/start-here/how-it-works/)
- Front as asset property (DA Snap): connection arrow must point outward from the module — [DA: Connections](https://docs.dungeonarchitect.dev/unreal/snap-flow/snapflow-connections/)
- Stretch flag per module (PCG): Scalable — [Epic: Using Shape Grammar with PCG](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/using-shape-grammar-with-pcg-in-unreal-engine)
- Stretch by size kind per rule (CGA): `~` floating sizes and `*` repeat — [Esri CGA: split](https://doc.arcgis.com/en/cityengine/latest/cga/cga-split.htm)
- Gap filler module (Houdini): sideslop — [SideFX: Labs Building Generator 4.0](https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/nodes/sop/labs--building_generator-4.0.html)
- Pivot conventions differ and are corrected rule-side: CGA ignores asset pivot in default fit — [Esri CGA: i](https://doc.arcgis.com/en/cityengine/latest/cga/cga-i.htm); PCG assumes centre pivot and needs an offset otherwise — [Epic: Fence Generator tutorial](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/creating-a-fence-generator-using-shape-grammar-in-unreal-engine)
- Edits kept by living upstream: Townscaper stores only blocks and colours — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/how-townscaper-works-a-story-four-games-in-the-making); DA seed determinism and separate landscape edit layers — [DA: How it works](https://docs.dungeonarchitect.dev/unreal/start-here/how-it-works/); Houdini per-floor overrides — [SideFX: Labs Building Generator 4.0](https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/nodes/sop/labs--building_generator-4.0.html)

### Inferences
- Two families of contract. Grammar/marker systems (CGA, PCG grammar, DA themes, Houdini, Buildify) need from an asset only a bounding size and an agreed axis convention, and put role, facing and stretch in the rule. Constraint/snap systems (WFC, DA Snap) need the asset to carry its own interface: geometry exact at cell faces, or explicit oriented connectors.
- Typed sockets are rare in shipped tools. Only DA Snap has explicit connectors and its typing was not confirmed; WFC "sockets" are either pair lists or derived from geometry. No system examined publishes a socket type vocabulary.
- Three recurring answers to variable-length walls: (a) integer repeat with slight uniform stretch of every piece (CGA floating repeat, Buildify); (b) repeat rigid pieces and absorb the remainder in one designated stretchable or filler piece (PCG Scalable, Houdini sideslop); (c) quantise the world to a grid so no remainder exists (WFC, DA grid builders, Townscaper).
- Three recurring answers to corners: (a) cut/mitre geometry at face boundaries (CGA trim planes); (b) dedicated convex and concave corner assets (Houdini, Buildify); (c) corner-centric modules where the corner is the primitive (Townscaper).
- Hand edits survive only as: edits to the generator's input (blockout mesh, spline, block grid), keyed overrides (per floor, per shape attribute), or by detaching output from the generator. No system examined was confirmed to merge free edits of generated output back into a regenerated result.

### Gaps
- Hand-edit persistence for CityEngine (local edits), PCG and Houdini beyond per-floor overrides is unverified in this pass; these are the claims a report writer should source before relying on them.
- Typing of DA Snap connections and the exact geometry-matching rule in Stålberg's games are unverified.

## 8. Vendor-neutral standards for building-kit metadata or building grammars

### Takeaway
No vendor-neutral standard for modular-kit metadata (role, size, sockets, stretchability) or for building grammars was found; each system's rule format is its own. Open standards exist only for the results (CityGML, IFC) and for the mesh containers (glTF, USD, FBX, OBJ).

### Cited Findings
- CityGML 3.0 is an open OGC standard for semantic 3D city models (conceptual model with GML, JSON or database encodings), with improved interoperability with INSPIRE and IFC building models; it describes buildings, not kits or generation rules **[search summary]** — [GIM International on CityGML](https://www.gim-international.com/content/news/ogc-adopts-city-geography-markup-language-v2-0), [OGC](https://stage24.ogc.org/?p=2481)
- Procedural/shape-rule approaches are used in research to produce CityGML models (for example generating LoD2+ from LoD2), i.e. grammars sit outside the standard and emit into it **[search summary]** — [ISPRS Annals IV-4/W8, 2019](https://isprs-annals.copernicus.org/articles/IV-4-W8/83/2019/isprs-annals-IV-4-W8-83-2019.pdf)
- CityEngine's `i()` consumes open mesh formats (glTF 2.0, USD, IFC 2x3, OBJ, DAE, FBX) but ignores FBX custom attributes and reads only UsdGeomMesh from USD, so no kit metadata travels with the asset even where the container could hold it — [Esri CGA: i](https://doc.arcgis.com/en/cityengine/latest/cga/cga-i.htm)
- Each tool's rule language is distinct: CGA text rules — [Esri CGA: split](https://doc.arcgis.com/en/cityengine/latest/cga/cga-split.htm); PCG grammar strings — [Epic](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/using-shape-grammar-with-pcg-in-unreal-engine); DA theme graphs — [DA](https://docs.dungeonarchitect.dev/unreal/start-here/how-it-works/); WFC XML tilesets — [mxgmn](https://github.com/mxgmn/WaveFunctionCollapse)

### Inferences
- The nearest thing to a shared vocabulary is conceptual, not a file format: (symbol, size along an axis, stretch flag) appears independently in CGA and in the PCG grammar, and (name, oriented frame) appears in DA markers, DA connections and Houdini instancing points. A project-specific kit schema built from those two tuples would map onto every system examined.
- glTF `extras`/extensions or USD custom attributes could carry such metadata, but no consuming tool examined reads them, so any such schema would be self-defined.

### Gaps
- Absence of a standard is a negative finding from one search plus the tools' own docs; not exhaustive. Not checked: glTF extensions registry, USD schemas (for example any AOUSD work), OGC CDB, Esri rule packages (.rpk) as a de facto exchange format, academic grammar interchange proposals.
