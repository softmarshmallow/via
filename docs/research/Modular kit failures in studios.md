# Kit failures hide until the layout closes

Studios that ship large worlds from modular kits assemble them by hand, placing instances of pieces that share a footprint, a pivot rule and a door size, and almost every failure they describe is a failure of that shared contract which the first join does not reveal. It shows when a layout loops back and misses by half a module, when two storeys are stacked and the floor turns out to have no thickness, when a second designer uses a piece in a way the first pair never tried, or when a whole city has to fit together and one moved building pushes its neighbours. The consistent repair was to fix the offending piece or rule and refuse the extra "patch" piece; where something was added, it was a shared interface (one door size, a glue kit, a socket that takes any plug) or a prefab layer that can be taken apart again. Tool builders report the same thing from the other side: building generators fail on the strictness of the piece contract, on corners, on roofs and on ground that is not flat, and the constraint solvers that shipped had large-scale structure taken away from them and were left only the choice of piece. Player-facing builders have spent years loosening their snapping rules after launch. For a text-first assembler that places instances only, this is mostly good news, because its author already states the structure, but it sets firm requirements: closure checks that run without art, refusals that name the piece and the rule, declared fallbacks where other tools stretch, and placements recorded against named joints so a revised piece can be accounted for. The evidence is uneven. Bethesda's interior kits are documented first-hand, its Fallout 4 talk only through slide text and one note-taker, Embark's building system first-hand; roofs on irregular plans, buildings on slopes, East Asian roofs, classical orders and industrial exteriors have little or no developer testimony. Sections 1 to 12 keep sourced findings apart from this report's reasoning, which is always marked; section 13 turns the accounts into examples to rebuild; section 14 says what could not be read.

Evidence flags used throughout. Unmarked claims are a developer's own text or a tool's own documentation, on a page that was opened. **(slides)** means the bullet text of the 2016 Fallout 4 talk, with no speaker notes. **(notes)** means a third party's notes on a talk, chiefly one Russian write-up of that same talk. **(summary)** means the claim was seen only in a search-result summary and the page was not opened. **(player)** means a player or user report, not a developer. **(hobbyist)** means an author or forum poster with no shipped credit stated. **(vendor)** means vendor documentation or a marketplace listing. Most pages were read through a fetch tool that summarises them, so wording attributed to sources is paraphrase; the exceptions, read in full, are Joel Burgess's 2013 and 2014 talk transcripts and his 2018 interview. Paragraphs that begin *Reasoning, not sourced* are this report's own.

## 1. "Kitbashing" names a design method and a banned shortcut

Bethesda's Joel Burgess and Nate Purkeypile define a kit as a system, not an asset list: four pipe shapes that give unlimited runs, pieces that share one footprint, one pivot rule and one set of transitions ([2013 transcript](http://blog.joelburgess.com/2013/04/skyrims-modular-level-design-gdc-2013.html)). Against that, they use "kitbashing" in two senses that they treat in opposite ways. In the first, also called kit-jamming or a kit mashup, pieces from two kits that were not designed together are combined to make a third look, as when Dwemer halls meet ice caves; this is to be **actively encouraged** as a defence against repetition ([2013 transcript](http://blog.joelburgess.com/2013/04/skyrims-modular-level-design-gdc-2013.html)). In the second, "kitbashing or arthacks" means using a piece for a job it was not made for, such as a wall turned on its side as a bridge. They ship with many such hacks, but in the first layout pass they are **discouraged, because a hack that early hides a gap in the kit** that should stay visible and be fixed in the kit ([2014 transcript](http://blog.joelburgess.com/2014/07/gdc-2014-transcript-iterative-level.html)). A third sense, bashing small parts into new objects, appears in Fallout 4's "Machine Kit" of greebles, fins and tanks: a level designer blocks out the machine's space and an artist assembles the final object from parts ([2016 slides](https://archive.org/stream/GDC2016Burgess/GDC2016-Burgess_djvu.txt)) (slides). The write-up of the talk says about 400 objects were made this way against roughly two weeks for one unique asset; the two readings of that write-up disagree on whether they were machines or cars ([dtf.ru](https://dtf.ru/gamedev/3788796-modulnyi-dizain-fallout-4)) (notes). Generic glossaries define kitbashing only in this third sense, and one adds that an approved kitbash is usually rebuilt as a single clean mesh afterwards ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitbashing); [nhance glossary](https://nhance-school.com/tools/glossary/kitbashing)) (summary).

Mixing worked where the result could be loose or where a shared transition existed, and failed at clean architectural joins. The failure modes the authors name for unforeseen combinations are **bad intersections and lighting problems**, and their remedy was review of actual use plus ambient occlusion to hide the joins ([2013 transcript](http://blog.joelburgess.com/2013/04/skyrims-modular-level-design-gdc-2013.html)). Purkeypile says that once the kits were loosened he had to review every dungeon so results did not look wrong ([The Examined Game](https://www.theexaminedgame.com/confessions-of-a-bethesda-veteran-nate-purkeypile-fallout-3-4-76-skyrim-starfield/)) (read as a tool summary). A cave corner hidden by a floor-to-ceiling insert had seams of its own, which were then hidden by rock columns, so the fix ran several layers deep ([dtf.ru](https://dtf.ru/gamedev/3788796-modulnyi-dizain-fallout-4)) (notes; the slides confirm "layered inserts" as a technique). Outside Bethesda the one named cost of heavy modularisation is from PUBG: more scene objects, which raises draw calls and collision counts ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/creating-diverse-buildings-for-pubg/)). No practitioner source that was opened describes kitbashing breaking collision, navigation or the ability to swap pieces; that absence is a gap, not a finding.

*Reasoning, not sourced: what this asks of the tool.* Three different contracts sit under one word. Mixing kits needs a contract between kits (section 6). Using a piece against its declared role needs to be visible, so the tool should tell "two kits joined through a declared transition" from "a piece used where its role does not allow", and should warn on the second instead of forbidding it, since studios ship with such uses once the kit is proven. Bashing parts into an object needs almost no contract but should end as one named assembly (section 5), or the result cannot be swapped.

## 2. Footprint, pivot, snap rule and door size freeze first, and no studio describes a migration

Every source that discusses revision names the same properties as unchangeable: the footprint, the pivot, the snap rules and the door or transition metrics. Changing a pivot late means **manual updates to hundreds of instances**, and it is worse if the pivot has already changed once ([2013 transcript](http://blog.joelburgess.com/2013/04/skyrims-modular-level-design-gdc-2013.html)). Five years later Burgess widened the list to pivot location, base footprint size and transition metrics, and the scale to hundreds or thousands of layouts ([80.lv 2018](https://80.lv/articles/building-huge-open-worlds-modularity-kits-art-fatigue/)). A kit bug is unlike an ordinary asset bug: an obvious fix to a bad pivot cures the reported case and creates new problems in hundreds of placements nobody thought to check. The mechanism that causes this is also the benefit, since each export reaches every level at once and an artist sees new art in real use with no test area ([2013 transcript](http://blog.joelburgess.com/2013/04/skyrims-modular-level-design-gdc-2013.html)). The human cause, in Burgess's account, is that an artist does not see why moving a tiling edge matters, because it looks like visual polish and is functional change ([80.lv 2018](https://80.lv/articles/building-huge-open-worlds-modularity-kits-art-fatigue/)). The write-up of the 2016 talk adds that this had happened at Bethesda more than once when a modeller updated a pivot without knowing the piece was already in levels, though it is unclear whether that sentence is the speakers' or the note-taker's ([dtf.ru](https://dtf.ru/gamedev/3788796-modulnyi-dizain-fallout-4)) (notes).

**No source describes a tool or script for re-fitting layouts after a kit change.** The defence is entirely procedural. Function is frozen on untextured proof pieces before visuals; one visually complete piece is built before any variants, so an art-direction change costs one piece and not a kit; level designers are promised that pivots and similar functional details will stay put while visuals evolve ([2013 transcript](http://blog.joelburgess.com/2013/04/skyrims-modular-level-design-gdc-2013.html); [2014 transcript](http://blog.joelburgess.com/2014/07/gdc-2014-transcript-iterative-level.html)). Fallout 4 made this a gate: layout may start when the core kit is usable, and kit work aims at being trustworthy in function ([2016 slides](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/gdc-2016-modular-level-design-of-fallout-4/59770460)) (slides). Metrics that many kits depend on are fixed game-wide before any kit begins: one door-frame size, the narrowest walkable width, the slopes characters can climb and the slopes that look right ([2013 transcript](http://blog.joelburgess.com/2013/04/skyrims-modular-level-design-gdc-2013.html)). The authors admit the promise does not always hold, that pieces arriving very late are hard to work back into built spaces, and that they budget polish time for corrections because later users always find what the first designer and artist missed. Before this discipline existed, design sat idle waiting for art, art rushed its decisions and then changed them, and level work was thrown out. Their own caveat is that the approach makes a designer wholly dependent on the kit: without one she cannot start ([2013 transcript](http://blog.joelburgess.com/2013/04/skyrims-modular-level-design-gdc-2013.html)).

Two accounts from outside Bethesda show the same hazard at the scale of a settlement. At Warhorse, **enlarging the houses after the first three quests were built broke the first quest**, because its characters no longer behaved in the larger interiors; the speaker's lesson is that an open world has no closed level that can be finished and locked ([interview mirror](https://fextra.pages.dev/posts/kingdom-come-deliverance-interview-with-warhorse-development-update-dlc-taking-chances-building-a-community)). Todd Howard says world scale was often slightly wrong early and changing it mid-development was a problem, so the team now sketches the world on paper and walks the topography first ([Making of Fallout 4 statements](https://fallout.wiki/wiki/Fallout_4_Developer_Statements/The_Making_Of_Fallout_4)) (read as a tool summary). Generator-based pipelines answer differently. Embark keeps shared dimensions such as wall thickness and floor height once, on the building, where every feature node reads them; a change recooks only what depends on it, and a building goes from blockout to finished asset in four to six minutes ([SideFX](https://www.sidefx.com/community/making-the-procedural-buildings-of-the-finals-using-houdini/)). Techland's city tool can push a change of building style or geometry across the whole city ([Wccftech](https://wccftech.com/the-big-dying-light-2-qa-techland-opens-up-on-c-engine-tech-delays-game-length-much-more/)).

Some early decisions have no free answer, and here the evidence drops to forum posters and hobbyists. Putting the wall's centre-line on the grid line needs fewer pieces and has fewer edge cases, but the wall then eats the room and exact interior sizes go off the grid; putting the wall to one side needs many more parts ([Polycount](https://polycount.com/discussion/232991/creating-modular-walls-with-thickness-how-to-handle-corners-and-alignment)) (hobbyist). Sources disagree outright about where the pivot goes (ground-plane centre for Burgess, one extremity for Thiago Klafke, the footprint centre for one hobbyist, a lower corner in Roblox's kit) and agree on the rule above it: one convention per kit, identical among pieces meant to swap, never changed ([Klafke](https://www.thiagoklafke.com/modularenvironments.html); [Ian London](https://ianlondon.github.io/posts/modular-level-kit-geometry); [Roblox docs](https://create.roblox.com/docs/tutorials/3D-art/assembling-modular-environments)). Off-ratio pieces, such as a 192-unit piece in a 128-unit set or a 2.5 m wall beside 2 m walls, are the mistake both Klafke and a Polycount thread describe; the thread's fix, a finer snap, contradicts Burgess's rule that the snap should be as coarse as possible so misalignment shows at once ([Polycount](https://polycount.com/discussion/comment/2661655)) (hobbyist). No source gives a cost in hours or weeks for any late change, and no incident is told first-hand with a kit name and a date.

*Reasoning, not sourced: what this asks of the tool.* The sources split a piece's properties into two classes without naming them: functional (footprint, pivot, edges that tile, door metrics, role) and visual (everything inside the footprint and away from its edges). A tool can make that split explicit, pass visual revisions silently and list every placement a functional revision touches. Because studios never built a migration, there is room for one: a placement recorded against a named joint or cell, and not against a raw transform, can be recomputed when a pivot moves. The Warhorse story adds a second requirement. The damage came from content that depended on a house's size without saying so; a tool should let dependants (a door position, an interior marker, a path) refer to declared dimensions, so that a changed dimension can name them.

## 3. Loops, scale and the second user expose what a pairwise join hides

The clearest accounts share one property: the kit passed when two pieces were joined and failed later. A room on a 384-unit footprint snaps to a 256 or 512 hallway at first and **fails only when the layout loops back**, with gaps or overlaps; the rule drawn is that sub-kit footprints must be multiples of one base, and a non-square piece is acceptable only if its run returns to the base grid ([2013 transcript](http://blog.joelburgess.com/2013/04/skyrims-modular-level-design-gdc-2013.html); [2016 slides](https://archive.org/stream/GDC2016Burgess/GDC2016-Burgess_djvu.txt)). In a room-and-hallway loop test the last door missed by about half a footprint. The first reaction was to ask for a half-length hallway, which the authors call a patch-up piece: it fixes one layout, bloats the kit and adds a usage rule nobody will guess. The real fault was a corner piece longer on one side than the other, and the fix was to rework that piece to sit inside the footprint ([2013 transcript](http://blog.joelburgess.com/2013/04/skyrims-modular-level-design-gdc-2013.html)). Burgess repeated in 2018 that patch pieces subvert a kit's logic ([80.lv 2018](https://80.lv/articles/building-huge-open-worlds-modularity-kits-art-fatigue/)). A hallway whose alcoves poked outside the footprint could not be placed beside another; rather than give designers an arbitrary layout restriction, the piece was redrawn with the alcoves inside. Asymmetric "directional" pieces, begun in Fallout 3, arrive flipped when a run loops back and need a dedicated de-twist piece to swap sides; the authors judged this worth it for organic kits and usually not for architectural ones, and concluded that a kit should bend at most one rule ([2013 transcript](http://blog.joelburgess.com/2013/04/skyrims-modular-level-design-gdc-2013.html)).

The second user is the other detector. Designers build pillared rooms out of hallway corners and junctions, producing pillars the artist never designed; somebody always does, so the pair who own a kit must decide early what it supports and what it does not. A Fallout 3 sub-kit carried its joining rule in a name token that was meant to say what a piece tiles with on each side. **Nobody could read it, so the kit was underused** and part of the effort of building it was lost; the authors annotate the token as undecipherable in their own slide ([2013 transcript](http://blog.joelburgess.com/2013/04/skyrims-modular-level-design-gdc-2013.html)). The later answer moved compatibility information out of names and onto the piece as editor-only helper markers: outlines of walls and doorways, the way a door opens, which side of an asymmetric hall is which ([2013 transcript, comments](http://blog.joelburgess.com/2013/04/skyrims-modular-level-design-gdc-2013.html); [dtf.ru](https://dtf.ru/gamedev/3788796-modulnyi-dizain-fallout-4)) (the shipped form is known from notes). A graybox that leaves out whatever must continue across footprints, such as a pipe down the middle of a room, proves nothing. The authors also say people who receive a finished kit through the released editor find it arbitrary, because the collaborator who knew its limits does not ship with it ([2013 transcript](http://blog.joelburgess.com/2013/04/skyrims-modular-level-design-gdc-2013.html)).

Other failures appeared only at scale. Fallout 4's Utility kit was built whole as an asymmetric one-way room kit; the slide's verdict is that it was not really worth it, because all the team used were its pipes, which survived as a kit that joins other kits ([2016 slides](https://archive.org/stream/GDC2016Burgess/GDC2016-Burgess_djvu.txt)) (slides; detail from notes). Fourteen planned kits became fewer when five were folded into one Building kit, on the reasoning that every kit carries lead time, on-boarding, iteration and maintenance ([2016 slides](https://archive.org/stream/GDC2016Burgess/GDC2016-Burgess_djvu.txt)) (slides). Boston is the largest case. Fallout 3's city districts were walled-off cells, easy to cut and reshape; Fallout 4's were interconnected, everything had to fit, and the slide's summary is that everything took longer than expected ([2016 slides](https://archive.org/stream/GDC2016Burgess/GDC2016-Burgess_djvu.txt)) (slides). According to the write-up, each neighbourhood was owned by one designer and one artist, the model **broke at the borders** where a change in one district forced changes in the next, and the first plan of using buildings as whole meshes failed ([dtf.ru](https://dtf.ru/gamedev/3788796-modulnyi-dizain-fallout-4)) (notes). The same write-up says this moved the release by a year; no second source supports that figure and it should be treated as unverified. Elsewhere, PUBG's team found bare, hard corners only after buildings were assembled from modules in the engine ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/creating-diverse-buildings-for-pubg/)); Embark found that thirty-odd enterable buildings with freely varied interiors confused players and replaced the variety with one interior rule ([SideFX](https://www.sidefx.com/community/making-the-procedural-buildings-of-the-finals-using-houdini/)); and Bad North's generated islands sometimes could not be crossed, with a navigability check added late that wrongly assumes every tile is walkable inside ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/how-townscaper-works-a-story-four-games-in-the-making)).

The tests Bethesda names (loop the kit back on itself, stack it, rotate groups to see the gaps, try the misuse) are all run by hand by a level designer on untextured pieces, and the designer is told that testing a kit only as intended teaches nothing ([2013 transcript](http://blog.joelburgess.com/2013/04/skyrims-modular-level-design-gdc-2013.html); [The Level Design Book](https://book.leveldesignbook.com/process/blockout/metrics/modular)). A forum thread adds S-shaped layouts as the test for thick-wall corner sets ([Polycount](https://polycount.com/discussion/232991/creating-modular-walls-with-thickness-how-to-handle-corners-and-alignment)) (hobbyist). No account of an automated kit test in a studio was found.

*Reasoning, not sourced: what this asks of the tool.* Loopback, stack, gap and S-shape are closure tests: build a shape that must return to its start and measure whether it does. They need footprints, not art, so a tool can run them mechanically from declared facts. Pairwise compatibility is not evidence that a kit is sound. When a run needs a filler to close, the right output is a defect report against the kit that names the piece whose extent disagrees with its footprint, not a suggestion to add a half piece. And the PRTT story is a direct result about people: joining rules that live in names or in someone's head are not used, so they have to be carried and checked by the tool.

## 4. Corners, stacking, roofs and the ground: testimony thins in that order

### Corners need their own pieces, or a cover

Thick walls make the corner a separate problem. With the wall set inside the grid line, an inside corner overlaps itself and an outside corner leaves a notch; the answers offered are a dedicated inside-corner piece, bevels or caps outside, and pieces per angle plus three-way and four-way junctions ([Ian London](https://ianlondon.github.io/posts/modular-level-kit-geometry); [Polycount](https://polycount.com/discussion/232991/creating-modular-walls-with-thickness-how-to-handle-corners-and-alignment)) (hobbyist). The one shipped account is PUBG's: once buildings were assembled from separate meshes the corners could not be smoothed across module boundaries, and **about half of building-creation time went into small supplementary assets**, chiefly corner pieces, a stone base and roof pieces ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/creating-diverse-buildings-for-pubg/)). Generators report the same spot. Users of the SideFX Labs building node describe corners that overlap in some places and gap in others, traced by one replier to corner modules whose own depth pushes or pulls the adjoining walls; no SideFX staff answered either thread ([SideFX forum](https://www.sidefx.com/forum/topic/72937/?page=1); [Project ZERO devlog](https://tubbytobe.itch.io/project-zero/devlog/877134/devlog-2-290125)) (user reports). Buildify breaks if its pillars are not listed in a fixed order of flat, concave, convex, and Unreal's grammar documentation warns that tight corners on a spline can come out wrong ([Buildify documentation](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1xOR41G5D2-gxJGu3MyR5Oi-2VJvoz7js11W7Cit-G-Q/mobilebasic); [Epic docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/using-shape-grammar-with-pcg-in-unreal-engine)).

Angles other than ninety degrees are never solved by making pieces meet. Bethesda's snap-to-reference lets a rotated piece become the local grid for what follows, which leaves gaps between groups; its "pivot and flange" method lets hall pieces turn at any angle and covers each joint with an archway. The costs listed are that the archway cannot snap to both halls, so designers misplace it and leave pinholes visible from one angle; that piece counts between two rooms stop being predictable, so loops no longer close and late adjustment is harder; and that clean intersections are impossible. They judged it right for a decayed sewer and used that kit twice ([2013 transcript](http://blog.joelburgess.com/2013/04/skyrims-modular-level-design-gdc-2013.html)). Burgess later named the trade: swinging pieces cost a kit its ability to tile on two horizontal axes ([80.lv 2018](https://80.lv/articles/building-huge-open-worlds-modularity-kits-art-fatigue/)).

### Stacking fails on floors without thickness and on stairs

A hall kit that used its whole vertical footprint gave a co-planar ceiling and floor when stacked: a paper-thin slab seen from any stairwell, flicker, and harder optimisation and trigger placement. The change was to decide floor thickness with the footprint. A kit that tiled on every axis proved too complex to build and to use, so vertical stacking got its own "shaft" sub-kit with a fixed horizontal size ([2013 transcript](http://blog.joelburgess.com/2013/04/skyrims-modular-level-design-gdc-2013.html)). Fallout 4's exterior building kit stacks in horizontal layers; the slides label a lobby level, lobby trim and roof trim, and the write-up gives the full stack as foundation, ground floor, a trim band, a repeating floor layer and the roof. The write-up also says pieces were allowed to overlap when stacking, which avoided naming and ordering rules ([2016 slides](https://archive.org/stream/GDC2016Burgess/GDC2016-Burgess_djvu.txt); [dtf.ru](https://dtf.ru/gamedev/3788796-modulnyi-dizain-fallout-4)) (notes for the full stack and the overlap). Stairs fit a module worst. One hobbyist offset his stair footprint by half a storey and doubts the design; another splits stairs into start, middle and end pieces ([Ian London](https://ianlondon.github.io/posts/modular-level-kit-geometry); [Beyond Extent](https://www.beyondextent.com/articles/balancing-modularity-and-uniqueness-in-environment-art)) (hobbyist). Tiny Glade's developers call stairs their largest change since launch: the shipped system was the fifth prototype, and the earlier ones were thrown away ([dev diary](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/2198150/announcements/detail/507328875549688875)). Vertical position also enters the contract in shipped builders. No Man's Sky made structural parts choose a foundation, middle or top variant from where they sit, and Rust made roofs occupy wall sockets to stop them being layered as armour ([Frontiers update](https://www.nomanssky.com/frontiers-update/); [Rust devblog 189](https://rust.facepunch.com/news/devblog-189)). Marian Kleineberg's generated city had missing roofs and holes in the ground until the top of the map was constrained to air and the bottom to solid ([marian42.de](https://marian42.de/article/wfc/)).

### Roofs depend on the whole plan, and most tools leave them out

This is where shipped-practitioner testimony about kits nearly stops. No account was found of pitched-roof kit pieces on an L-shaped or irregular plan from anyone who shipped a game. What exists on the kit side is a forum rule of thumb (build a roof like a hallway kit: middle, inner corner, outer corner, end cap), the remark that corners, roofs and foundations are the tricky parts of a building set, and PUBG's roofs arriving as supplementary pieces while one base building is reused under different roofs ([Unreal forums](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/designing-modular-roofs/156062/2) (hobbyist); [exp-points](https://www.exp-points.com/vuk-single-material-modular-kit-environment-ue4); [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/creating-diverse-buildings-for-pubg/)). Marketplace listings show roofs as a large share of a house kit, for instance twenty roof parts among forty-four pieces in one pack ([itch.io](https://mb-studio0.itch.io/low-poly-building-kit)) (vendor, summary).

The tool builders are more forthcoming, and what they describe is retreat. One Houdini tool shipped no roofs; Buildify supports only flat roofs and says others are hard because walls can meet at angles other than right angles; another tool's only automatic top is a ceiling mesh per floor ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/006sdf-environment-art-procedural-workflow-in-houdini); [Buildify documentation](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1xOR41G5D2-gxJGu3MyR5Oi-2VJvoz7js11W7Cit-G-Q/mobilebasic); [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/procedural-buildings-in-ue4-logic-behind-the-tool)). Epic's City Sample places rooftops as ready-made assemblies in a separate step after the buildings ([Epic docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/city-sample-pcg-for-unreal-engine?lang=en-US)). Embark's general roof node builds from four artist meshes (plane, ridge, fascia, eaves); **layered Japanese roofs did not fit it, and the team wrote a separate roof node** so one style's constraints would not leak into the default ([SideFX](https://www.sidefx.com/community/making-the-procedural-buildings-of-the-finals-using-houdini/)). Adam Newgas (Boris the Brave) found from experience that tile-based roofs do badly on arbitrary shapes and switched to a straight skeleton, which then needed extra libraries and its own answer for texture coordinates ([For Keep's Sake explained](https://www.boristhebrave.com/2020/12/12/for-keeps-sake-explained)). Gaslamp Games spent about two years on a weighted straight skeleton whose roofs blew apart, and rewrote it to compute the whole wavefront before building any mesh ([Gaslamp](https://archive-gaslamp.dredmor.com/?p=6161)). The author of Island Architect lists how misaligned ridges should join as an open question ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/island-architect-townscaper-inspired-city-builder-on-custom-engine)). In Tiny Glade the gable was the trickiest part of half-timbered buildings, and the unsightly seam where a tiled roof met a flat one was fixed by generating a row of merlons over it ([dev diary, July 2024](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/2198150/announcements/detail/6294426665445590929); [dev diary, January 2024](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/2198150/announcements/detail/3977309173904464147)). The abstract of an Epic talk from 2010 already names trim, roofs and non-rectangular faces as the hard cases; the talk itself was not read ([GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1012219/Building-Blocks-Artist-Driven-Procedural)).

### Meeting the ground has almost no testimony

The only shipped evidence about kits is that a foundation layer sits under Fallout 4's facade stack (notes), that PUBG added a sculpted stone base under its modular buildings, and that Bethesda's platform kits are made to sit on other surfaces ([dtf.ru](https://dtf.ru/gamedev/3788796-modulnyi-dizain-fallout-4); [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/creating-diverse-buildings-for-pubg/); [2013 transcript](http://blog.joelburgess.com/2013/04/skyrims-modular-level-design-gdc-2013.html)). On the tool side, **flat ground is a stated precondition**: Buildify needs a completely flat base, one Houdini tool handles only flat or slightly uneven ground, and Tiny Glade's arch generator, written when the meadow was flat, could not handle elevation and was rewritten once terrain existed ([Buildify documentation](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1xOR41G5D2-gxJGu3MyR5Oi-2VJvoz7js11W7Cit-G-Q/mobilebasic); [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/006sdf-environment-art-procedural-workflow-in-houdini); [dev diary](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/2198150/announcements/detail/3682298662732600152)). The small adaptations that are described are substitutions: a door with no ground under it becomes a balcony in one Houdini tool, and Tiny Glade's stairs become platforms and ladders as steepness rises ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/procedural-environment-for-unity-with-houdini); [stairs update](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/2198150/announcements/detail/561377319446381084)). Nothing was found from a practitioner on foundation skirts on slopes, steps up to doors, terraces or stilts.

*Reasoning, not sourced: what this asks of the tool.* A corner needs a declared extent along both walls, and a wall run must be measured after its corners are subtracted; the Labs reports are what happens otherwise. A cover piece (pillar, flange, merlon row, ridge) is a piece whose contract is "hides a join of uncertain fit", and every account that hides a seam uses one, so it deserves to be a role. Storey position belongs in the role a piece plays (base, ground, middle, top, roof) and should not be guessed from height. For roofs, an instance-only tool cannot use a straight skeleton, which produces geometry. The options the evidence supports are a piece decomposition for plan shapes that have one (rectangle, L, T), a whole-roof model keyed to a class of footprint, a separate rule set per roof style, and an explicit refusal for everything else. No source reports kit-piece roofs succeeding on a plan that is not right-angled. For ground, the default visible in two shipped examples is a base piece tall enough to sink into the terrain, with substitution where a door or stair loses its footing; that is this report's reading, since no source states it.

## 5. Piece size swung from rooms to wall panels, then back to prefabs that come apart

Bethesda's history runs through three sizes. In Oblivion, with half Skyrim's team, the dungeon artists made "warehouse" cells of fully lit, cluttered rooms to copy and rearrange; players called the result cookie-cutter, and the studio abolished warehouse cells from Fallout 3 on and built from the most granular art available ([2013 transcript](http://blog.joelburgess.com/2013/04/skyrims-modular-level-design-gdc-2013.html)). Through Skyrim a hall corner was still exported whole, as floor, two walls and ceiling in one mesh, which the 2016 slides call a lot of redundant work: each request for the door and window the other way round was a new exported piece ([2016 slides](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/gdc-2016-modular-level-design-of-fallout-4/59770460); [dtf.ru](https://dtf.ru/gamedev/3788796-modulnyi-dizain-fallout-4)) (the combinatorial example is from notes). Fallout 4 broke the corner into floor, wall, wall-corner and ceiling pieces. The gains listed are freedom for destruction, interchange between kits and layouts that no longer depend on combinations chosen in advance. The cost on the slide is that **the same simple room went from 20 placed objects to 123**. The repair was the "pack-in": an editor prefab of granular pieces, named as the old whole piece was, placed as fast as before, and able to be exploded when a designer needs to swap one member ([2016 slides](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/gdc-2016-modular-level-design-of-fallout-4/59770460)) (slides). According to the write-up's account of the questions, the engine combines pack-in meshes for performance, so the team stopped worrying about piece count ([dtf.ru](https://dtf.ru/gamedev/3788796-modulnyi-dizain-fallout-4)) (notes).

Exteriors moved the same way under different pressure. Whiterun's houses were large modules usable only in that city and often joinable in one way, and Fallout 3's downtown pieces were very large; for Boston's irregular streets the buildings were remade from much smaller modules, several times more work, which let artists bend building shapes to the street ([dtf.ru](https://dtf.ru/gamedev/3788796-modulnyi-dizain-fallout-4)) (notes). Other shipped teams choose size by distance and repetition. PUBG balances shared pieces against draw calls and collision counts and reuses one base building under different roofs; an Arkane artist finds the facade's unit by looking for repeating structural elements and makes only distant versions of whole buildings; Klafke uses small modules for pavement, large ones for facades and merged ones outside the playable area ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/creating-diverse-buildings-for-pubg/); [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/modular-design-in-dishonored-2); [Klafke](https://www.thiagoklafke.com/modularenvironments.html)). Several pipelines stop at the building. Assassin's Creed Origins used a modeller-made library of whole houses plus modular parts such as balconies, roofs and windows; The Sinking City went from a library of elements to prefabs of the buildings typical of a neighbourhood type to a drawn plan that says which type goes where; Rise of the Ronin places whole building assets on blocks ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/creating-the-world-of-assassins-creed-origins/); [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/ue4-tool-behind-the-streets-of-the-sinking-city); [CGWORLD](https://cgworld.jp/article/202410-cgw313-rotr02.html)).

Unique pieces come last. A hero piece is used a few times and a standard piece hundreds or thousands of times, so the order is utility pieces, then variants, then hero pieces, and designers are asked to prove a need with placeholders first ([2013 transcript](http://blog.joelburgess.com/2013/04/skyrims-modular-level-design-gdc-2013.html); [2016 slides](https://archive.org/stream/GDC2016Burgess/GDC2016-Burgess_djvu.txt)). The write-up's example is the Boston Public Library: the designer's advance list of unique pieces turned out replaceable by standard ones, and the reading room's windows and ceiling, which nobody had listed, did not ([dtf.ru](https://dtf.ru/gamedev/3788796-modulnyi-dizain-fallout-4)) (notes).

*Reasoning, not sourced: what this asks of the tool.* Prefabrication failed at the level of dressed, lit, closed rooms and was brought back at the level of bare structural groups that can be opened. The pack-in is the piece of this craft closest to a language feature: a named assembly that is one thing to place and count, whose members stay addressable so one can be swapped or damaged, and which is re-derived when a member piece is revised. No source says how pack-ins were kept in step with their members, which is the question a text-first tool can answer by construction. The Boston story adds a size rule: a coarse piece is tolerable only where its surroundings can be reshaped freely, so the unit must be small enough to absorb a local change without pushing it outward.

## 6. Kits meet through one door size, glue kits, and sockets that take any plug

Kits with unrelated logic cannot be joined, and Bethesda's answers form a progression. First, one door-frame size for the whole game lets kits meet without a unique transition piece per pair and gives animation and AI a single standard ([2013 transcript](http://blog.joelburgess.com/2013/04/skyrims-modular-level-design-gdc-2013.html)). In Fallout 4 the single and double door sizes were fixed at the start and taught to the whole team, since near the end everyone helps dress levels ([dtf.ru](https://dtf.ru/gamedev/3788796-modulnyi-dizain-fallout-4)) (notes; the slides confirm standardised transitions). Burgess later called a standard doorway, window and portal system the saving grace of a game with many distinct kits ([80.lv 2018](https://80.lv/articles/building-huge-open-worlds-modularity-kits-art-fatigue/)). Second, from the project after Skyrim the studio built small "glue" kits whose only job is to sit between kits and sub-kits: platforms, tunnels, ledges, and later the pipes rescued from the Utility kit ([2013 transcript](http://blog.joelburgess.com/2013/04/skyrims-modular-level-design-gdc-2013.html); [2016 slides](https://archive.org/stream/GDC2016Burgess/GDC2016-Burgess_djvu.txt)). Third, Fallout 4 added predefined "socket" holes that accept several "plug" pieces. Before that a new piece was made for each transition between two kit types; **a plug does not care which kit is on either side** ([2016 slides](https://archive.org/stream/GDC2016Burgess/GDC2016-Burgess_djvu.txt); [dtf.ru](https://dtf.ru/gamedev/3788796-modulnyi-dizain-fallout-4)) (slides for the mechanism, notes for the history). Damage followed the same thinking: the Building kit's wall holes are modelled as one side each, because the far side of the wall may belong to another kit ([dtf.ru](https://dtf.ru/gamedev/3788796-modulnyi-dizain-fallout-4)) (notes). Burgess states the catch: shared logic makes kits compatible and makes repetition more visible, so kits that replace each other or combine heavily should share logic and isolated environments should not ([80.lv 2018](https://80.lv/articles/building-huge-open-worlds-modularity-kits-art-fatigue/)). The socket dimensions, and which kits implemented them, are given nowhere that could be read.

The tool literature supplies the warnings. Kleineberg replaced hand-written neighbour lists with numbered connectors per face and then had to add explicit exclusion rules, because **some pieces whose connectors match still look wrong together** ([marian42.de](https://marian42.de/article/wfc/)). Oskar Stålberg found that windows authored into tiles came out cut in half or stuck on corners, and moved windows to a step after placement ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/how-townscaper-works-a-story-four-games-in-the-making)). Embark's opening module is three things: the visible mesh, a second mesh that cuts the hole, and sockets where the engine spawns actors ([SideFX](https://www.sidefx.com/community/making-the-procedural-buildings-of-the-finals-using-houdini/)). On the player side, Starfield's ship builder decides for itself where doors and ladders appear between joined modules; players reverse-engineered its preference order and a popular mod lets doors be placed by hand ([Dot Esports](https://dotesports.com/starfield/news/starfield-ship-building-door-and-ladder-placement-explained)) (player, summary).

*Reasoning, not sourced: what this asks of the tool.* Each step in Bethesda's progression reduces the number of pieces that must know about two kits at once: from a piece per pair, to a shared opening size, to an interface any kit can implement. A kit contract therefore needs typed openings whose size is declared once for a world, pieces that declare which opening type they fill, and a way to veto specific pairs that match on paper. Features that cross a piece boundary (a window band, a pipe run) should not be baked into pieces. Embark's hole-cutting mesh is not available to a tool that places instances only, so an opening has to be a piece that replaces a wall bay, or a socket left by the wall piece's own geometry. The Starfield complaint is the PRTT failure seen from the user's side: a join produced a third thing by a rule the author could neither see nor steer.

## 7. Leftover length: stretching is the default, and the answer its authors apologise for

When a run is not a whole number of modules, the sources give ten answers. The table lists each with what its own author or documentation says about it.

| Stretch every piece, always | Buildify | Cannot be turned off; the documented workaround for games is to snap the footprint to multiples of the unit ([docs](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1xOR41G5D2-gxJGu3MyR5Oi-2VJvoz7js11W7Cit-G-Q/mobilebasic)) |

*Reasoning, not sourced: what this asks of the tool.* For a tool that never deforms a model without leave, the real choices are four: constrain the run to the kit, substitute a declared narrower variant, insert a declared filler, or stop and report the closing lengths. Stretch can exist only as a per-piece permission, as in Unreal. Substitution is the one answer nobody regrets, and it needs each role to declare its fallbacks. The Subveillance lesson generalises: settle the remainder at a coarse level, in whole modules, and expand afterwards, so slivers cannot arise.

## 8. Players notice repeated dressing first, then the lattice itself

Bethesda's observation from Oblivion screenshots is that **repeated clutter is noticed before repeated architecture**, especially in first person, and that with play times over a hundred hours players see the same rock or farmhouse dozens of times ([2013 transcript](http://blog.joelburgess.com/2013/04/skyrims-modular-level-design-gdc-2013.html)). A second kind of repetition is the grid: a rules-based kit always feels like a kit because everything sits at right angles, which is what pushed the studio to bend rules from Fallout 3 on. Purkeypile recalls an earlier studio's game where the modularity was obvious because everything faced one of four compass directions ([dtf.ru](https://dtf.ru/gamedev/3788796-modulnyi-dizain-fallout-4)) (notes). Cube-shaped footprints are the most versatile and look the most repetitive ([80.lv 2018](https://80.lv/articles/building-huge-open-worlds-modularity-kits-art-fatigue/)). Reader comments under the 2013 transcript say the grid gives every hallway, door and street the same proportion ([2013 transcript, comments](http://blog.joelburgess.com/2013/04/skyrims-modular-level-design-gdc-2013.html)) (player).

The remedies that Bethesda says worked are these. A kit is kept as architecture only, not tied to one enemy type or mood. Variants share role and footprint and carry a numeric suffix so they sort together and swap in place, and Fallout 4 added a mouse-wheel swap to the next variant ([2013 transcript](http://blog.joelburgess.com/2013/04/skyrims-modular-level-design-gdc-2013.html); [2016 slides](https://archive.org/stream/GDC2016Burgess/GDC2016-Burgess_djvu.txt)). Material swaps change surface only, so the geometry need not be tested again ([The Level Design Book](https://book.leveldesignbook.com/process/blockout/metrics/modular)). Damage, which repeats worst, was split into many small pieces for a multiplicative effect; the write-up's detail is eleven wall-hole halves giving 121 pairings ([2016 slides](https://archive.org/stream/GDC2016Burgess/GDC2016-Burgess_djvu.txt); [dtf.ru](https://dtf.ru/gamedev/3788796-modulnyi-dizain-fallout-4)) (notes for the count). Floor and ceiling damage could not be done with variants of whole tiles and was handled by free platforms raised and lowered independently, and decals and small added parts are listed as useful to obscure kit patterns ([2016 slides](https://archive.org/stream/GDC2016Burgess/GDC2016-Burgess_djvu.txt)) (slides).

Generators report the same two layers. Stålberg shelved his first tile builder because its output became repetitive on anything but a small grid ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/how-townscaper-works-a-story-four-games-in-the-making)). Newgas notes that local constraints make large outputs uniform, that the cures are different tile sets per region or a subset per level, and that variants with identical connections add variety at no cost to the solver ([WFC tips and tricks](https://www.boristhebrave.com/2020/02/08/wave-function-collapse-tips-and-tricks/)). Team Ninja's rule for Edo was that identical buildings may not stand side by side; after feedback, houses were also shifted and distorted to remove the generated look ([CGWORLD](https://cgworld.jp/article/202410-cgw313-rotr02.html)) (machine-translated). Embark met the opposite fault: variation between neighbouring interiors was itself the problem, and the fix was sameness by rule ([SideFX](https://www.sidefx.com/community/making-the-procedural-buildings-of-the-finals-using-houdini/)). There is no measurement of what players notice anywhere in these sources, only designers' observation.

*Reasoning, not sourced: what this asks of the tool.* Repetition has two layers that need different answers. Repeated instances are answered by variants and swaps, which are safe only if the tool checks that variants share footprint, pivot and role. A visible lattice is answered only by changing the joining rule (local grids, cover pieces, free-placed layers); variants do nothing for it. The remedies that scale are products, not sums: split a visible feature along a line the contract does not care about and choose each half independently. A neighbour rule ("not the same as the one beside it") and its inverse ("the same arrangement in every building of this group") are both real requirements.

## 9. Solvers kept the choice of piece and lost the structure

Three authors who built constraint solvers for buildings name the same failures: contradictions that grow with the size of the output, sameness at scale, and no way to state intent. Stålberg's Bad North restarts an island that breaks its constraints and still occasionally loads only after a long wait; **long, thin pieces such as walls and pipes are what the method handles worst**; and in Townscaper the solver is allowed to fail silently, which sometimes leaves loose steel supports hanging ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/how-townscaper-works-a-story-four-games-in-the-making)). Kleineberg's infinite city could not be generated chunk by chunk because one choice restricts cells several steps away; a straight tunnel piece with no entrance piece implied an infinite tunnel; dead ends first showed as a white block and then, with backtracking, occasionally regenerated the cell the player stood in. His conclusion is that the method is unsuitable for commercial games with infinite worlds ([marian42.de](https://marian42.de/article/wfc/)). Newgas states that contradictions are a fact of any constraint-based approach, that with restarts large outputs become practically impossible, and that no commercial game uses the algorithm unmodified ([Constraint-Based Tile Generators](https://www.boristhebrave.com/2021/10/31/constraint-based-tile-generators/); [Tessera paper](https://pcgworkshop.com/archive/newgas2021tessera.pdf)). His paper singles out one complaint: when generation fails there is no clear explanation, and his own tool's FAQ concedes the question is very hard to answer in general ([Tessera FAQ](https://www.boristhebrave.com/docs/tessera/6/articles/faq.html)).

What they changed has one direction. Newgas calls it "driven" generation: decide the large structure another way and let the solver only select pieces that realise it. That is how he characterises Townscaper, where the user says which cells are solid, and Kleineberg's later city, where a coarse heightmap constrains every eighth tile ([Driven WaveFunctionCollapse](https://www.boristhebrave.com/2021/06/06/driven-wavefunctioncollapse)). The other working answers are a known-good fallback arrangement to return to after too many failures, nice-to-have goals moved out of hard constraints into the order of choice, and a fixed priority order that makes output deterministic ([Model Synthesis and Modifying in Blocks](https://www.boristhebrave.com/2021/10/26/model-synthesis-and-modifying-in-blocks/); [Constraint-Based Tile Generators](https://www.boristhebrave.com/2021/10/31/constraint-based-tile-generators/)). Minecraft's village structures reportedly use a fallback pool of terminating pieces when a chosen piece cannot be placed ([Minecraft Wiki](https://minecraft.fandom.com/wiki/Custom_world_generation/template_pool)) (summary).

Building generators retreated in the same way. Embark concluded its tool had to be a set of building blocks and not a rigid generator, because the maps span very different styles ([SideFX](https://www.sidefx.com/community/making-the-procedural-buildings-of-the-finals-using-houdini/)). One Houdini author built a random mode first and then had to add a manual mode because the level designer needed control ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/procedural-environment-for-unity-with-houdini)). The developer of Shadows of Doubt designs floor plans by hand and generates only the subdivision inside each ([devblog](https://colepowered.com/?p=34642)). CD Projekt Red tested procedural tools for Night City and judged them short of the quality wanted, keeping one spline road tool ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/interview-how-cyberpunk-2077-s-night-city-was-built-almost-entirely-by-hand/)). Where layout had to look right it was supplied as data: Team Ninja's automatic streets came out straight and unnatural, so the road network was drawn from period maps of Edo and blocks were generated from it, and Warhorse takes village layouts from nineteenth-century military maps ([CGWORLD](https://cgworld.jp/article/202410-cgw313-rotr02.html); [Xbox Wire](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2025/02/04/building-an-authentic-bohemia-in-kingdom-come-deliverance-2/)). No detailed postmortem of a studio abandoning a working building generator was found; the retreats are in scope, from generator to toolset and from random to authored with generated fill.

*Reasoning, not sourced: what this asks of the tool.* A text-first tool in which the author states the structure already stands on the side these authors moved to. What remains is to keep the rest of their lessons: a plain piece per opening type that is guaranteed to fit, so there is always a floor to fall back to; continuity and termination as their own rule for long thin runs (pipes, rails, walls), which should not be left to adjacency; deterministic choice, with no random tie-breaking; and failure reports that name the piece, the joint and the rule, which is the one thing the solver authors say they could not give.

## 10. Player-facing builders spent years loosening their snapping

Shipped games keep relaxing their snapping contracts after launch. Fallout 76's 2025 revision added a mode without auto-snap and a free mode without snapping or collision, and dropped a list of old requirements: pieces that had to snap may be left unsnapped, two pieces may share one snap position, walls may go under a floor, upper floors may attach without a staircase, things that needed a foundation no longer do, and removing a piece no longer risks stranding part of a structure ([C.A.M.P. Revamp release notes](https://fallout.bethesda.net/en-US/news/fallout-76-c-a-m-p-revamp-release-notes)) (first-party, read as a tool summary). The notes do not say why the old rules existed. Valheim's automatic snapping made pieces jump between nearby points, and its developer showed manual snapping in which **the player cycles which point of the piece takes priority** ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/valheim/building-snapping-update)). Satisfactory split one clearance rule into hard clearance for functional buildings and soft clearance for cosmetic and structural pieces, belts and pipes, which may now overlap with a warning ([patch notes](https://satisfactory.wiki.gg/wiki/Patch_0.5.0.0)). No Man's Sky added free placement in which parts may intersect and still be valid ([Frontiers update](https://www.nomanssky.com/frontiers-update/)). Rust went the other way on one point, tightening sockets to close exploits, and its earlier rebuild was driven by art cost, since every component had needed seven stage models ([devblog 189](https://rust.facepunch.com/news/devblog-189); [devblog 34](https://rust.facepunch.com/news/devblog-34)).

The gridless systems pay differently. Tiny Glade's developers describe chains of bespoke algorithms, a general system for elevated buildings that had to place brackets, supports and pillars automatically, and features that went through five or six iterations; they say there will always be cases they have not thought of ([dev diary](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/2198150/announcements/detail/3977309173904464147); [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/exclusive-tiny-glade-developers-discuss-bevy-proceduralism-publishers-cozy-games)). Island Architect's author frames the trade: free placement leaves awkward gaps, a regular grid is rigid, and his adaptive grid aligns curved buildings at the price that buildings cannot stack inside each other ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/island-architect-townscaper-inspired-city-builder-on-custom-engine)). Several further items rest on weaker evidence. Players of Fallout 4 complain that pieces snap everywhere except where wanted ([Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/377160/discussions/0/496881136907750951/)) (player, summary). An indie prototype found one shared list of snap positions wrong, since pillars should snap to floor corners and not edges, and tagged each position with the structure type it serves ([Vercidium](https://www.patreon.com/vercidium/posts/huge-post-new-98247640)) (summary). Satisfactory reportedly added a world-wide foundation grid after years of early access ([patch notes mirror](https://redlib.cypherpunktools.com/r/SatisfactoryGame/comments/qr0msq/patch_notes_early_access_experimental_v0508_build)) (summary). The Fallout 4 workshop gave players under a hundred pieces against roughly five hundred for developers ([dtf.ru](https://dtf.ru/gamedev/3788796-modulnyi-dizain-fallout-4)) (notes). **No developer account was found** of how snap points were designed or went wrong in Fallout 4's workshop, Starfield's outposts or ship builder, Enshrouded, Fortnite, Cities: Skylines II or Manor Lords.

*Reasoning, not sourced: what this asks of the tool.* The Fallout 76 list reads as an inventory of an old contract (mandatory snap, one occupant per position, support from below, foundation required, stairs required, no intersection, connectivity on removal), each rule presumably blocking legitimate builds more often than bad ones; that is a reading of release notes, not a developer statement. Three games converged on one repair for ambiguous snapping: do not guess, let the author pick. In text, an ambiguous join should be an error or an explicit choice, never a silent nearest point. Clearance wants two classes, pieces that must not overlap and pieces that may. Connection points want types, so a pillar is offered corners and a wall is offered edges.

## 11. Hand edits survive as inputs, as anchored overrides, or by freezing the output

Three designs are reported to survive regeneration, each with a stated limit. Embark stores viewport edits as simple geometry in parameters on the tool, **in world space and not as references to element numbers** in the generated geometry, so an edit keeps applying as long as its feature stays in the same place; the post does not say what happens when the feature moves ([SideFX](https://www.sidefx.com/community/making-the-procedural-buildings-of-the-finals-using-houdini/)). Townscaper stores only the player's input, hidden layers of solidity and colour, and derives everything visible from them in a fixed priority order, so the same input always gives the same town ([Editable WFC](https://www.boristhebrave.com/2022/04/25/editable-wfc/)) (third-party analysis). Newgas's own method for editable solvers re-solves toward the previous result, re-picking only cells whose old tile is no longer possible; he says it needs a similarity measure that may not exist, that it is often impossible to know in advance which cells will change, and that plain regeneration returns a level with nothing in common with the last ([Editable WFC](https://www.boristhebrave.com/2022/04/25/editable-wfc/)). Tiny Glade recomputes only the regions an edit touches ([dev diary](https://steamcommunity.com/games/2198150/announcements/detail/5970156536060713335)).

The other documented behaviours are wholesale. In Epic's City Sample, changing a lower-numbered graph rebuilds every later one, and changing lot or zone settings regenerates building volumes and lots ([Epic docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/city-sample-pcg-for-unreal-engine?lang=en-US); [Epic docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/city-sample-quick-start-for-generating-a-city-and-freeway-using-houdini)). Ghost Recon Wildlands lost manual edits at the next procedural update unless the data was locked first, and the system that followed Marvel's Spider-Man keeps hand-authored work that still meets its specification and flags the rest ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/procedural-technology-in-ghost-recon-wildlands/); [SIGGRAPH 2019 abstract](https://history.siggraph.org/learning/procedural-system-assisted-authoring-of-open-world-content-for-marvels-spider-man-by-santiago/)). Unreal can unlink generated output so it stops regenerating and becomes ordinary content, and a paper from Esri's team proposes locating each edit by its local spatial context ([Epic API docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/API/Plugins/PCG/UPCGComponent/ClearPCGLink); [Computer Graphics Forum 2019](https://diglib.eg.org/items/3b6d6f2a-6b98-4f68-9b54-03033d917da2/full)) (both summary). Team Ninja shifted and distorted houses after generation; the article does not say whether those changes survive another run ([CGWORLD](https://cgworld.jp/article/202410-cgw313-rotr02.html)). No first-hand account was found of hand changes being wiped by a re-run in production; the evidence is the designs built to prevent it.

*Reasoning, not sourced: what this asks of the tool.* World-space anchoring and index anchoring fail in opposite cases: the first survives reordering and not a moved building, the second survives a move and not a changed piece count. An override anchored to a named element in the source text plus a position within it (storey, bay, side) survives both, at the price of stable names. Storing only input avoids the problem by having no output edits, and works until someone wants a per-element exception, as Embark's artists did. "Unlink and freeze" is the escape every pipeline keeps, so a building's state (derived, overridden, frozen) should be visible. Determinism matters as much as identity: any random tie-break turns each re-run into a possible lost edit. Bethesda's ban on arthacks in the first pass is the same principle from the kit's side: an early local fix hides a defect the source should carry.

## 14. How far each part of this report can be trusted

The strongest material is Bethesda's interior kits: the 2013 and 2014 transcripts and the 2018 interview are the speakers' own text and were read in full. Everything about what was said in the 2016 Fallout 4 talk rests on slide bullets plus one Russian note-taker whose wording could not be checked against the recording; this covers the Utility kit detail, the combinatorial case for granular pieces, the pack-in naming, sockets replacing per-pair transitions, the whole Boston narrative beyond three slides, the facade stack, the claim that pieces may overlap when stacking, and the one-year delay, which has no second source. Embark's account is first-hand and detailed but is one studio's blog post on a vendor site. The tool-builder accounts (Stålberg through an interview, Kleineberg, Newgas, Tiny Glade's diaries, Gaslamp) are first-hand; the Labs and Unreal corner problems are unanswered user reports. Guidance on wall thickness, corner sets, stairs and pivots comes mostly from hobbyists and forum posters. Roof piece counts are marketplace listings seen in summaries. The player-builder section mixes first-party patch notes with press and player reports, and its Fallout 76 inference reads motive into a change list. ## Conclusion

The accounts change what a kit assembler should be judged on. Studios did not suffer from placing pieces; they suffered from contracts that were unwritten, unreadable or unchecked, and their craft is a set of manual substitutes for a checker: untextured proof pieces, a designer paid to break the kit, a rule against early hacks, a review of every dungeon. A text-first tool starts where they ended, with structure authored and pieces chosen, and can do by measurement what they did by discipline. Its distinctive ground is the part no source describes anyone having: a kit check that runs closure tests from declared facts, a refusal that explains itself, and an account of what a revised piece touches.

The same accounts mark where the tool should not promise much. Roofs on plans that are not right-angled, buildings that follow slopes, and the structural grammar of East Asian and classical architecture have no body of shipped practice to copy, and the tool builders who tried the general case mostly withdrew to flat roofs, per-style rules or whole hand-made assemblies. Examples in those areas will be tests of the tool's own design with nothing to check it against, and they should be labelled as such when they are built.
