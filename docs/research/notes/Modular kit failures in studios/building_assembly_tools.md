# Building-assembly tools: what failed and what the builders changed

Provenance tags used below. **[first-hand]**: written or said by the people who built the system, and I opened the page. **[docs]**: the tool's own documentation, opened. **[third-party]**: someone else's account, opened. **[search summary]**: I only saw a search-result summary; the page was not opened or did not load. Anything under "Inferences" is mine.

Pages that could not be opened are listed in the Gaps of the question they belong to.

## 1. Production building generators: what do their authors say failed?

### Takeaway
The failures authors report are rarely about the placement algorithm. They are about the kit's contract being stricter than artists expect (one module width, watertight input, a fixed pivot), corners that overlap or gap, flat ground only, a generator too rigid for more than one architectural style, and tools polished in private that nobody then used. Roofs, ceilings and terrain are the features most often left unfinished.

### Cited Findings

**Embark Studios, Building Creator for THE FINALS and ARC Raiders (Houdini), post dated 11 Feb 2026 [first-hand]**
- The tool was deliberately not one all-in-one generator. It is a set of interoperable HDAs ("Feature Nodes" for walls, floors, roofs, windows), so a bug can be isolated to one node and changing a window does not recook the whole building. The author's conclusion is that it had to be a toolset of building blocks and not a rigid generator, because the maps span very different architectural styles. — [SideFX: Making the procedural buildings of THE FINALS](https://www.sidefx.com/community/making-the-procedural-buildings-of-the-finals-using-houdini/)
- The general Roof node could not express layered traditional Japanese roofs for the Kyoto map. The fix was a separate Kyoto Roof node, so that one style's constraints did not leak into the default node. — [same](https://www.sidefx.com/community/making-the-procedural-buildings-of-the-finals-using-houdini/)
- With 30+ buildings of one to five floors plus attic on the Monaco map, interiors felt unpredictable. The fix was a fixed interior ruleset (consistent entrance, staircase, exit and attic placement) so a player who learns one building understands the rest. Furniture was removed because sprinting players snagged on it. — [same](https://www.sidefx.com/community/making-the-procedural-buildings-of-the-finals-using-houdini/)
- Early component names were loose and game-specific and made the structure hard to read. They were renamed after real architectural terms. — [same](https://www.sidefx.com/community/making-the-procedural-buildings-of-the-finals-using-houdini/)
- Custom modules (visual mesh, boolean mesh, mesh sockets) work only if the input mesh is prepared to the tool's expectations, and destruction requires fully watertight input. The author describes this as a mindset change for artists. — [same](https://www.sidefx.com/community/making-the-procedural-buildings-of-the-finals-using-houdini/)
- Floor slabs are simply not generated when the available height is below a threshold. — [same](https://www.sidefx.com/community/making-the-procedural-buildings-of-the-finals-using-houdini/)
- On adoption, the author's lesson is to release early: "The fastest way to make an unused tool is to over-polish it before showing it." Most of the best ideas came from the Maps team. Reported result: 4 to 6 minutes from blockout to fractured asset, 100+ unique buildings across two games. — [same](https://www.sidefx.com/community/making-the-procedural-buildings-of-the-finals-using-houdini/)
- For ARC Raiders, which has no destruction, the tools generated only base building meshes and details were placed as instanced static meshes because that was more efficient. — [same](https://www.sidefx.com/community/making-the-procedural-buildings-of-the-finals-using-houdini/)

**Feike Postmes, Houdini tool built at Square Enix Advanced Technology Division, Jan 2020 [first-hand, interview]**
- Roofs were not done; he calls them the most interesting but also a difficult feature. Ceilings were dropped to save time (visible as holes under balconies). The tool handles only flat or slightly uneven ground. Kit assets must follow fixed rules (the plane where the object meets the wall, correct sizing) or the building does not fill properly. The tool sets an element count per facade and scales the elements slightly to fit. — [80.lv: Environment art procedural workflow in Houdini](https://80.lv/articles/006sdf-environment-art-procedural-workflow-in-houdini)

**EXE Collective, Houdini tool for UE4, Dec 2019 [first-hand, interview]**
- Each mesh set needs at least three wall meshes, two corners and a floor. All meshes must share one width, ideally a multiple of 2 m, and follow strict naming and folder rules or the tool does not recognise them. The grid cell size is derived from the straight-wall width, and corners go wherever the drawn curve changes direction. Doors can go only at positions listed by ID. The only automatic "roof" is a ceiling mesh on top of each floor. Terraces come from trimming floors by hand. — [80.lv: Procedural buildings in UE4, logic behind the tool](https://80.lv/articles/procedural-buildings-in-ue4-logic-behind-the-tool)

**Buildify (Blender geometry nodes), "Known issues and limitations" [docs]**
- Needs a completely flat base surface; tilted surfaces give wrong results. Overlapping edges flip wall orientation, the author does not know how to fix it, and the workaround is to nudge the edge by a tiny amount. Only one wall module size per building; a model with the wrong dimension makes the system fail. Only flat roofs. Slightly curved walls get a pillar between every segment unless a threshold is tuned. Pillars must sit in one collection in a fixed order (flat, concave corner, convex corner) or instancing breaks. No support for more complex structure logic; that must be baked into a single prefab. — [Buildify documentation](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1xOR41G5D2-gxJGu3MyR5Oi-2VJvoz7js11W7Cit-G-Q/mobilebasic)

**SideFX Labs Building Generator, user reports (no SideFX staff replied in either thread) [third-party]**
- Holes at building corners that the poster could not close. A replier traced it to corner modules whose slab dimensions push or pull the adjoining walls, and to the rule that the corner at the origin must match across all three modules. The poster reported the tutorial's steps did not reproduce and that the tutorial file differed from its text. Another user called the node "less usable" and asked SideFX for smaller basic modelling nodes instead of a comprehensive one. — [SideFX forum, April 2020](https://www.sidefx.com/forum/topic/72937/?page=1)
- In "Building from Patterns", a user reported that the convex/concave corner inputs were broken (filling the first box produced both corners, the second had no effect), traced it to both corner variables reading the same index in the grammar-expansion code, then posted two successive fixes, retracting the first because it ignored relative corner sizes. — [SideFX forum, Oct 2022 to Sept 2023](https://www.sidefx.com/forum/post/402071/)
- An indie developer using the same node says it works with minimal effort but corners overlap in some places and leave gaps in others, calls this common with the node, found no fix after repeated crashes, and was considering writing a custom generator. — [Project ZERO devlog, 29 Jan 2025](https://tubbytobe.itch.io/project-zero/devlog/877134/devlog-2-290125) [first-hand, indie]

**Unreal PCG shape grammar [docs and forum]**
- The documentation warns that on Subdivide Spline tight corners may be underestimated and produce incorrect results. — [Epic docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/using-shape-grammar-with-pcg-in-unreal-engine)
- A user could not get sharp corners from a grammar on a linear spline; nobody answered, and the user's own workaround was to cut the spline into one small spline per straight run with Blueprint and feed those to the grammar. — [Epic forum, Jan 2025](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/pcg-grammar-and-linear-spline-and-sharp-corners/2281327) [third-party]
- A user called the grammar powerful but not artist-friendly because the whole rule is one text string, and asked for an indexed list of mesh, size and position instead. No Epic reply is visible. — [Epic forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/feedback-pcg-new-grammer-feature-isnt-user-friendly/2077438) [third-party]
- Epic's City Sample PCG page: buildings are among the most resource-intensive graphs, pausing PCG while iterating is strongly recommended, each building gets its own actor (the exception to partitioning), collision must be enabled by hand, and the grammar rules require manual mapping of dozens of static meshes. Rooftops are spawned as ready-made static mesh assemblies in a separate step after the buildings. — [Epic docs: City Sample PCG](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/city-sample-pcg-for-unreal-engine?lang=en-US)

**Smaller first-hand accounts**
- Marina Bade's Shanty Town (Houdini Engine for Unity, 2018): she built random mode first, then found the level designer needed far more control and added a custom mode with typed box sizes. Doors were the hardest element to place (wall size, obstructing neighbours, a raycast for ground). Randomly scattered windows looked wrong, so one window per face. — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/procedural-environment-for-unity-with-houdini)
- Anastasia Opara's 2016 Houdini building tools: modules depend on each other in a hierarchy (a window enables a frame, a balcony enables a drying rack), and placement should follow a sensible direction of dependence; as she puts it, a chimney should not drive the placement of the whole building. Offsets and rotations were added only after the main relationships held. — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/realistic-procedural-architecture-for-games/)

### Inferences
- Every tool here that takes user-made pieces ends up publishing a mesh contract (one width, pivot location, naming, order in a collection), and each reports that breaking it fails silently or badly. A kit check that runs before assembly and names the offending piece addresses the most common complaint.
- Corners are where the wall contract and the corner contract meet: the Labs reports show a corner piece's own depth changing where adjoining walls end. A corner needs a declared size along both walls, and the wall run must be computed after corners are subtracted.
- Embark's split into per-feature nodes and a separate node per roof style suggests that style-specific rules belong in separate, replaceable rule sets and not as switches on one generator.
- Flat ground is an explicit precondition in three of the tools (Buildify, Postmes, and Tiny Glade's first arch generator, see section 5). Slope support was added late or not at all.

### Gaps
- I found no GDC or Houdini HIVE talk transcript in which a studio describes its building generator failing; talk videos could not be read. The Matrix Awakens "Generating a World" talk is known only from a summary that gives no failure details. — [80.lv summary](https://80.lv/articles/breakdown-creating-the-matrix-awakens-in-houdini-unreal-engine-5)
- A LinkedIn article "Houdini Building Generator: Part 2" returned HTTP 403 and was not read.
- No first-hand account was found for CityEngine used in a game, nor for Dying Light 2's City Builder (Techland's blog post does not describe the tool). — [Techland blog](https://techland.net/blog/the-development-process-behind-dying-light-2s-environments-and-buildings)
- A forum comment that the City Sample Houdini project was "broken since launch" appeared only in a search summary and is a single unanswered user post. [search summary]

## 2. The remainder problem: stretch, filler, or fail, and what went wrong with each

### Takeaway
Four answers recur: stretch pieces, restrict the footprint so there is no remainder, substitute a smaller variant, or report failure. Stretching is the default in the tools that accept arbitrary footprints and is the one authors apologise for; restricting the input is what the tools aimed at games actually recommend.

### Cited Findings
- **Stretch, always on.** Buildify stretches walls to fit the building shape and this cannot be turned off; the documented workaround for games is to snap the footprint to multiples of the grid unit so nothing stretches. — [Buildify documentation](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1xOR41G5D2-gxJGu3MyR5Oi-2VJvoz7js11W7Cit-G-Q/mobilebasic) [docs]
- **Stretch, opt-in per piece.** In Unreal PCG, only modules that still fit in the remaining space are considered, and leftover space is filled by stretching modules marked Scalable; non-scalable modules are never stretched. If nothing fits, the node raises an incomplete-subdivision warning, which the user can accept ("Accept Incomplete Subdivision") or silence ("Silence Grammar Does Not Fit Errors", hidden under Advanced). — [Epic docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/using-shape-grammar-with-pcg-in-unreal-engine) [docs]
- **Count, then scale slightly.** Postmes' tool picks the element count per facade and scales elements a little to close the gap. The article does not say what happens at large mismatches. — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/006sdf-environment-art-procedural-workflow-in-houdini) [first-hand]
- **Remove the remainder by constraining input.** EXE Collective derive the grid from the wall width and require every mesh to share it. — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/procedural-buildings-in-ue4-logic-behind-the-tool) [first-hand]. Boris the Brave's castle generator discards any foundation with an angle under 35 degrees, which he admits excluded many more interesting shapes. — [For Keep's Sake explained](https://www.boristhebrave.com/2020/12/12/for-keeps-sake-explained) [first-hand]
- **Restricting dimensions rejected.** The Subveillance developer considered limiting building sizes to "friendly" values that avoid slivers and rejected it because the outcomes were too hard to predict; also rejected detect-and-regenerate because a low yield would leave players waiting. The fix was to treat the generated map as a coarse zoning pass in which each square expands to a 4x4 block, which removed the narrow-strip cases as a class. — [Subveillance devlog, part 2](https://unusual-intersection.itch.io/subveillance/devlog/498365/procedural-generation-of-building-interiors-part-2) [first-hand, indie]
- **Substitute a smaller or different piece.** Tiny Glade: a chimney without enough space becomes a small vent pipe; a window atop a stone floor becomes a door; stairs become platforms and ladders depending on steepness. — [Dev diary, 2 Jul 2024, read via Steam's news feed](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/5842939267325165629); [Dev diary, 14 Mar 2024](https://steamcommunity.com/games/2198150/announcements/detail/5970156536060713335); [Stairs update, 29 Jul 2025](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/2198150/announcements/detail/561377319446381084) [first-hand]. Shanty Town: a door with no ground under it becomes a balcony. — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/procedural-environment-for-unity-with-houdini) [first-hand]
- **Skip.** Embark's tool omits a floor slab when the height is under a threshold. — [SideFX](https://www.sidefx.com/community/making-the-procedural-buildings-of-the-finals-using-houdini/) [first-hand]
- **Take space from a neighbour.** Shadows of Doubt lets a room that would be too small take floor area from an already-placed room that has excess. — [DevBlog 13, 28 Feb 2019](https://colepowered.com/?p=34642) [first-hand, indie]
- **No fixed pieces at all.** Tiny Glade assembles walls brick by brick, so there is no remainder, but the cost moved elsewhere: planks "need to be split up to fit with arches, windows/doors and various roof shapes", and horizontal planking is not offered on freeform walls because there is no good way to lay it on an arbitrarily curved surface. — [Dev diary: Wooden Walls, 11 Jul 2026](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/2198150/announcements/detail/684131849711125777) [first-hand]
- **Hide it.** A Polycount commenter says Skyrim's developers hid kit gaps with deco pieces. [search summary; third-party; page not opened] — [Polycount thread](https://polycount.com/discussion/159191/modular-design-issue)

### Inferences
- For an instance-only tool, stretch is not available without deforming, so the real choices are: constrain the footprint to the kit, substitute a declared narrower variant, insert a declared filler, or stop and report. Unreal's design (a per-piece Scalable flag plus an explicit "does not fit" error that must be consciously silenced) is the nearest precedent for "fail and ask".
- The substitution pattern (chimney to vent, door to balcony, stairs to ladder) is the only answer whose authors describe it without complaint. It requires the kit to declare fallbacks per piece role.
- Subveillance's lesson generalises: solving the remainder at a coarse level (zoning in whole modules) and expanding afterwards removes slivers by construction, where filtering dimensions or retrying does not.

### Gaps
- No author account was found of filler pieces going wrong in production (for example fillers stacking into a visible column). The Labs Building Generator threads concern corners, not fillers.
- No account was found of a tool that stops and asks the user interactively; the nearest is Unreal's warning.

## 3. Roofs: why they are described as the hardest part, what was tried, what was abandoned

### Takeaway
Roofs are hard because they depend on the whole footprint at once, while walls depend only on one edge. Module kits and tile grids both handle them poorly on non-rectangular or non-right-angle footprints, so authors either leave roofs out, restrict them to flat, give each style its own generator, or switch to a geometric method (straight skeleton) that then brings robustness and texturing problems of its own.

### Cited Findings
- **Left out or flat only.** Postmes shipped no roofs. — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/006sdf-environment-art-procedural-workflow-in-houdini). Buildify supports one roof type, flat, and says other roofs are difficult because buildings can have wall angles other than right angles. — [Buildify documentation](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1xOR41G5D2-gxJGu3MyR5Oi-2VJvoz7js11W7Cit-G-Q/mobilebasic). EXE Collective's only automatic top is a ceiling mesh per floor. — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/procedural-buildings-in-ue4-logic-behind-the-tool)
- **One roof generator per style.** Embark's general Roof node could not do layered Japanese roofs; they wrote a separate Kyoto Roof node. — [SideFX](https://www.sidefx.com/community/making-the-procedural-buildings-of-the-finals-using-houdini/) [first-hand]
- **Roofs as hand-made assemblies placed after.** Epic's City Sample PCG spawns rooftop assemblies from ready-made static mesh assembly assets in a step after the buildings. — [Epic docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/city-sample-pcg-for-unreal-engine?lang=en-US) [docs]
- **Tile roofs abandoned for straight skeleton.** Boris the Brave: a flat roof looked wrong for a gothic castle, and from experience tile-based approaches do not do a good job with roofs on arbitrary shapes. After some false starts he used a straight skeleton, which needed two extra libraries (polygons with holes, then the mesh). UVs were then the problem; he generated them by walking the perimeter. Lower roofs intersect the building, which hides seams but needed stronger ambient occlusion to hide the join; ridge pieces placed at sharp angles also hide UV seams. — [For Keep's Sake explained, Dec 2020](https://www.boristhebrave.com/2020/12/12/for-keeps-sake-explained) [first-hand]
- **Weighted straight skeleton: buildings "explode".** Gaslamp Games' Clockwork Empires generated a building from a player-drawn footprint using code based on Kelly and Wonka's procedural extrusions, modified over about two years. Roof geometry blew apart; artists also asked for more roof types, less fragility when cutting holes for doors, windows and modules, and better bevels. The rewrite stopped building geometry while the wavefront propagated: it computes the whole wavefront first, then builds the mesh. Debugging meant logging every propagation step to SVG and going through about seven rounds of failing buildings; colinear edges created after a split were a recurring undefined case. The stated result is that buildings "explode a lot less". — [How to Debug an Exploding Building, Nicholas Vining, 16 Oct 2013](https://archive-gaslamp.dredmor.com/?p=6161) [first-hand]
- **Roofs meeting walls at odd angles.** Björn Ottosson (Island Architect) calls wall and roof intersections at odd angles one of the most unexpected challenges and lists how misaligned roof ridges should join as an open design question. — [80.lv, 16 Jun 2025](https://80.lv/articles/island-architect-townscaper-inspired-city-builder-on-custom-engine) [first-hand, interview]
- **Tiny Glade.** Roofs started as tower roofs only, assembled from individual tiles; the developers did not expect roofs to matter much, but adding them immediately changed what people built. — [Dev update, 19 Dec 2022](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/2198150/announcements/detail/3631620304671700801). The gable was added later as a distinct roof type. — [Dev diary, 14 Mar 2024](https://steamcommunity.com/games/2198150/announcements/detail/5970156536060713335). For half-timbered buildings, "the trickiest part was making the gable roof", because the generators assemble brick by brick and cutting to the gable shape was hard. — [Dev diary, 15 Jul 2024](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/2198150/announcements/detail/6294426665445590929). Where a tiled roof met a flat roof there was an unsightly seam; the fix was to generate a row of merlons there. — [Dev diary, 30 Jan 2024](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/2198150/announcements/detail/3977309173904464147). A later hotfix was titled "Fix timber framing under roofs". — [Patch 1.13.2 listing, Steam news feed](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/1793384379510932) [all first-hand]
- **Missing roofs from a missing boundary rule.** Marian Kleineberg's WFC city produced buildings with missing roofs and holes in the ground until the top of the map was constrained to "air" connectors and the bottom to "solid". — [marian42.de](https://marian42.de/article/wfc/) [first-hand]
- **Auto-roof withdrawn.** The Sims 3 had optional auto-roofing; The Sims 4 removed it in favour of push-and-pull roof pieces, and players remain split. [search summary of The Sims Wiki and EA forum posts; third-party; pages not opened] — [The Sims Wiki: Roof](https://www.thesimswiki.com/wiki/Roof)
- **Academic note on the method's limit.** A plain straight skeleton treats the footprint as one shape, so it cannot roof parts of a building independently, and some roof shapes cannot be produced by it at all. [search summary of Sugihara's paper; not opened] — [Sugihara](https://dspace5old.zcu.cz/bitstream/11025/35614/1/Sugihara.pdf)

### Inferences
- The approaches fall into four groups, with this record: kit pieces per slope (works on rectangles, reported poor on anything else); straight skeleton (general, but fragile and produces generated geometry, not instances); hand-made roof per footprint or per style (Embark, City Sample: reliable, does not scale to arbitrary footprints); limit footprints (Buildify's flat-only, Boris's angle filter). No source reports kit-piece roofs succeeding on non-right-angle footprints.
- For an instance-only tool the workable options the evidence supports are: restrict roofed footprints to shapes with a known piece decomposition (rectangles, L, T), allow a whole-roof asset keyed to a footprint class, and treat everything else as flat or as an explicit refusal.
- Seams where two roof types meet are hidden with an added element (merlons, ridge pieces, intersecting volumes) in every account, never solved by making the surfaces match.

### Gaps
- No first-hand account was found of a studio trying straight-skeleton roofs in a shipped building generator other than Gaslamp.
- Watabou's city generator devlogs on gable roofs point to a Patreon post that was not read. — [itch.io devlog](https://watabou.itch.io/medieval-fantasy-city-generator/devlog/124980/city-viewer-101-gable-roofs-and-obj-export)
- Paralives' roof system being incomplete is known only from fan guides. [search summary]

## 4. Grid and constraint approaches: what failed and what they did

### Takeaway
Constraint solvers fail in three ways their authors all name: contradictions that grow with output size, sameness at large scale, and no way to state intent. The working answers are to decide the large structure elsewhere and use the solver only to pick pieces, to keep a known-good fallback arrangement, and to let rare failures show.

### Cited Findings

**Oskar Stålberg: Brick Block, Bad North, Townscaper [first-hand, interview]** — all from [Game Developer: How Townscaper works](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/how-townscaper-works-a-story-four-games-in-the-making)
- Brick Block's output became repetitive quickly; he kept the grid deliberately small and shelved the idea over the open question of how to get variety on a larger grid.
- Bad North: unchecked generation produced islands where units could not get from one side to the other, and no navmesh can exist for an island not yet built. He considered shipping pre-curated seeds and disliked it. He added a navigability heuristic late, and says it was not properly integrated: it assumes every tile is fully walkable inside, but sometimes there is a wall inside the tile.
- Long, thin pieces (walls, pipes) are what WFC handles worst; those tilesets failed often. Tall, small islands and later levels needing longer beaches were the hardest to generate.
- Islands that break hard or soft constraints trigger a restart from a state saved after the initial constraints. He says a level still occasionally seems broken and only loads after a long wait, a failure that "is still there".
- Houses are placed only in navigable areas; unnavigable areas get forest, which makes the restriction readable to the player.
- Townscaper: moving to an irregular quad grid meant box-shaped tiles no longer fit, so tiles became corner segments. WFC is allowed to fail silently instead of restarting, which occasionally leaves loose, unconnected structures, again mostly thin steel supports. Decoration can lag block placement because one change ripples through the connected area.
- Windows authored into tiles produced windows cut in half or stuck on corners; he moved windows to a stencil cut after placement. Gardens appear only in enclosed flat areas, and he calls the approach quite limited in what rules you can have. A reskin would need roughly 500 tiles.

**Marian Kleineberg, infinite WFC city [first-hand]** — all from [marian42.de](https://marian42.de/article/wfc/)
- Generating chunk by chunk failed: one collapse restricts slots several cells away, chunks did not propagate into neighbours, and the next chunk sometimes had no solution. He dropped chunks for a sparse map populated on demand, with constraints still propagating outside the visible area.
- A straight tunnel piece with no tunnel-entrance piece predetermines an infinite tunnel; the module set had to be designed to avoid that.
- Dead ends: first shown as a white block; then backtracking with stored history. Errors are sometimes found late, so many steps are undone and, rarely, the cell the player stands in is regenerated. His conclusion is that this makes WFC unsuitable for commercial games with infinite worlds.
- About 100 modules. Writing six neighbour lists per module by hand was the authoring problem; he replaced lists with numbered connectors per face (with flip and rotation symmetry) and generated the lists. Some pieces whose connectors match still look wrong together, so he added explicit exclusion rules on top.

**Boris the Brave (Adam Newgas): articles and Tessera [first-hand]**
- WFC only makes output look like the input locally, so levels lack global structure; fixes are fixed tiles for entrances and authored content, a path constraint that forces one connected component, and a drawn floor plan for WFC to fill. — [WFC tips and tricks, Feb 2020](https://www.boristhebrave.com/2020/02/08/wave-function-collapse-tips-and-tricks/)
- Large tilesets are hard to design; he recommends corner-matched tiles and leaving out rare tiles, since the solver plans around their absence. Unsupported structures are prevented by a tileset in which every tile's base is at least as wide as its top. Sameness is treated by splitting the map into regions with different tile sets (Caves of Qud) or by choosing a subset of tiles per level (Bad North). Variants with identical connections add variety at no constraint cost. — [same](https://www.boristhebrave.com/2020/02/08/wave-function-collapse-tips-and-tricks/)
- With restarts, large outputs become virtually impossible to complete. Backtracking replaces contradictions with stalls in unsolvable partial states. Modifying in Blocks solves overlapping blocks against a known-good arrangement (for example ground with air above) and falls back to it after too many restarts. Costs: it requires that known-good arrangement, has many tuning parameters, repeats work, and was sensitive to generation order. His own backtracking could not handle a 30x30x10 area. — [Model Synthesis and Modifying in Blocks, Oct 2021](https://www.boristhebrave.com/2021/10/26/model-synthesis-and-modifying-in-blocks/)
- "Driven WFC": decide the large-scale structure another way and use WFC only to select tiles that satisfy it. This is how he characterises Townscaper (the user draws which vertices are solid) and Kleineberg's later city (a coarse heightmap constrains every eighth tile). — [Driven WaveFunctionCollapse, Jun 2021](https://www.boristhebrave.com/2021/06/06/driven-wavefunctioncollapse)
- Contradictions are "a fact of any constraint based approach". Nice-to-have goals should go in the tile-choice heuristic and not be hard constraints. A priority-ordered tile choice, as in Townscaper, gives more deterministic and controllable output. — [Constraint-Based Tile Generators, Oct 2021](https://www.boristhebrave.com/2021/10/31/constraint-based-tile-generators/)
- Tessera paper: no commercial game uses WFC unmodified, because of homogeneous output, sample input that scales poorly as tiles and rotations grow, difficulty of control, and a weak solver. A common problem is that when generation fails there is no clear explanation; Tessera logs the contradiction's location and can step through guesses. Big tiles are implemented by splitting a module into cell-sized tiles with internal adjacency rules. Bad North is cited as having over 400 tiles and pre-selecting a subset per island. — [Tessera: A Practical System for Extended WaveFunctionCollapse, FDG 2021 (PDF)](https://pcgworkshop.com/archive/newgas2021tessera.pdf)
- Tessera's FAQ answers "why is my generation failing?" by saying the nature of the algorithm makes this very hard to answer in general, and notes that the tool handles naturally curving forms poorly and that local constraints make large outputs uniform. — [Tessera FAQ](https://www.boristhebrave.com/docs/tessera/6/articles/faq.html) [docs]
- In his own castle generator, quadrangulating an arbitrary outline robustly was hard and he "cheated" by slicing rows of quads; WFC coped well with missing tiles so he modelled only the necessary ones; extra constraints barred thin structures and required one contiguous castle. — [For Keep's Sake explained](https://www.boristhebrave.com/2020/12/12/for-keeps-sake-explained)

**Others**
- Shadows of Doubt: without a hallway from the entrance, rooms were reachable only through each other (a bathroom through several rooms); a hallway is now drawn toward a corner when the entrance is far. Room placement is scored (corner count for regular shape, windows, allowed connections, rooms barred from holding the front door) and the best-scoring spot wins. Floors are hand-designed in a separate floorplan tool and the generator only subdivides them. — [DevBlog 13](https://colepowered.com/?p=34642) [first-hand, indie]
- Dungeon Architect's snap builder: the developer has said modules connect only where door assets match and that doors should sit on the edge of the module's bounding box; users report vanishing snap points, missing doors between levels and a dungeon that never builds. [search summary of the Epic forum thread; the one page opened held only a user's report of nothing spawning and crashes from custom door models] — [Epic forum thread, post 1309](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/dungeon-architect/28094/1309)
- Minecraft's jigsaw structures use a fallback pool when a chosen piece cannot be placed or depth runs out, which is how village roads are closed off; if the fallback also fails, nothing generates. [search summary of the Minecraft Wiki; third-party; not opened] — [Minecraft Wiki: template pool](https://minecraft.fandom.com/wiki/Custom_world_generation/template_pool)

### Inferences
- The consistent move among people who shipped is to take structure away from the solver: the user or a coarse pass decides solid versus empty, and the solver only chooses which piece realises it. A text-first tool in which the author states the structure is already on that side of the line.
- Every robust design keeps a known-good default (Merrell's base arrangement, Minecraft's terminator pool, Tiny Glade's vent pipe). A kit that declares a plain piece guaranteed to fit any socket of a given type gives the assembler a floor to fall back to.
- "Connectors match but it looks wrong" (Kleineberg's exclusion rules) and "windows cut in half" (Stålberg) both show socket matching alone under-specifies a kit. Kits need both a socket contract and a way to veto specific pairs, and features that cross piece boundaries should not be baked into pieces.
- Thin, long pieces are named independently by Stålberg (twice) and guarded against by Newgas. They deserve their own rule class (continuity and termination) and should not be left to adjacency.
- Failure with no explanation is the complaint the Tessera paper singles out. Reporting which piece, which socket and which rule is a design requirement, not polish.

### Gaps
- None of Stålberg's talks (EPC 2018, Indiecade Europe 2019, "Beyond Townscapers" at Sweden Game Arena, Konsoll) could be read; they are videos and I found no transcript or detailed write-up. The 80.lv page on the EPC talk is a blurb. — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/using-wave-function-collapse-algorithm-for-dioramas)
- A PC Gamer interview with Stålberg loaded only its headline.
- Dungeon Architect's own documentation of snap failures was not found; the developer statements above are from a search summary.
- A 2023 arXiv paper that cites Kleineberg on backtracking regenerating existing parts was seen only in a search summary. — [arXiv 2308.07307](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.07307.pdf)

## 5. Player-facing building systems: snapping contracts under stress

### Takeaway
Shipped games keep loosening their snapping contracts after launch: letting the player choose which snap point wins, letting pieces overlap, and offering a mode with snapping off. The gridless systems (Tiny Glade) pay instead with many bespoke gluing rules and repeated rewrites when a new dimension (terrain, elevation, stairs) is added.

### Cited Findings

**Tiny Glade (Anastasia Opara, Tomasz Stachowiak) [first-hand]**
- There is no single algorithm: they describe entire chains of bespoke procedural algorithms feeding into one another. The arch generator, one of the oldest pieces of code, dated from when the meadow was flat; once terrain was added it could not handle elevation and was rewritten. — [Dev diary, 27 Apr 2023](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/2198150/announcements/detail/3682298662732600152)
- Building larger structures wall by wall with gridless drawing was cumbersome, so they added circle and rectangle shapes plus alignment and snapping helpers, after five discarded prototypes. — [Dev update, 21 Mar 2023](https://steamcommunity.com/games/2198150/announcements/detail/3681169040455721391)
- Bridges were not planned; playtesters kept trying to make them, and the answer became a general system for elevated buildings. That required further systems to "glue" pieces together (brackets, supports and pillars placed automatically and re-adapting when pieces move). They state there will always be cases they have not thought of given the freeform nature, and that they fix whatever stands out most. — [Dev diary, 30 Jan 2024](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/2198150/announcements/detail/3977309173904464147); same account in interview — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/exclusive-tiny-glade-developers-discuss-bevy-proceduralism-publishers-cozy-games)
- Players attached windows to corners in playtests, so corner windows became a feature; windows now also sit on roofs and on roof-wall edges and re-adapt when the roof shape changes. — [Dev diary, 14 Mar 2024](https://steamcommunity.com/games/2198150/announcements/detail/5970156536060713335)
- Some features went through five or six iterations. A request to place doors individually was hard because it touched the procedural system ("Doors, what does it mean?"). — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/exclusive-tiny-glade-developers-discuss-bevy-proceduralism-publishers-cozy-games)
- Stairs were the biggest and most complicated change since launch: several systems around object generation, attachment and bookkeeping were reworked, it was the fifth prototype, and earlier ones were dead ends thrown away. Before that, players made stairs by hand-aligning tiny wall pieces. — [Dev diary, 18 May 2025](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/2198150/announcements/detail/507328875549688875)
- Unsupported stairs are allowed and shown with a "magical" levitation effect; support can come from a building, a wall or other supported stairs. — [Stairs update, 29 Jul 2025](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/2198150/announcements/detail/561377319446381084)
- Recomputation is localised: drawing a path or terrain affects only some regions, and only those cause walls to recompute. — [Dev diary, 14 Mar 2024](https://steamcommunity.com/games/2198150/announcements/detail/5970156536060713335)

**Valheim [first-hand statement, reported by press]**
- Automatic snapping made pieces jump between nearby attachment points. Developer Jonathan Smårs showed manual snapping in Feb 2023, where the player cycles which point of the piece takes priority (stone bricks have eight), described as solving the old problem of building floor supports; holding Shift places from the chosen point with snapping off. — [PCGamesN, 10 Feb 2023](https://www.pcgamesn.com/valheim/building-snapping-update)

**Satisfactory [first-hand patch notes, re-hosted on the wiki]**
- Before Update 5 almost every building had a clearance that prevented building into another. The update split this into hard clearance (functional buildings still cannot overlap) and soft clearance (cosmetic and structural pieces, belts and pipes may overlap, with a warning that clipping may occur). Foundations gained 45-degree rotation with finer angles on a modifier. The notes list "many changes to the snapping" and ask players to report what got worse. — [Patch 0.5.0.0](https://satisfactory.wiki.gg/wiki/Patch_0.5.0.0)
- Foundation snapping kept being patched afterwards, including a change of the vertical snapping grid from 200 to 100; foundations near sloped terrain let terrain or grass clip through, solved only by building higher. [search summary of the wiki's Ramp page; not opened] — [Satisfactory wiki: Ramp](https://satisfactory.wiki.gg/wiki/Ramp)

**No Man's Sky, Frontiers 3.6 [first-hand patch notes]**
- Added a free-placement mode that disables snapping and lets parts intersect and still be valid, including in mid-air. Structural parts (walls, windows, arches) now choose a foundation, middle or top variant from their position, so top walls get trim and bottom walls get support beams. The old wood, concrete and metal sets were replaced by new sets and withdrawn from research. — [Frontiers update](https://www.nomanssky.com/frontiers-update/)

**Rust [first-hand devblogs]**
- The 2014 rebuild was motivated by art cost: under the old system every component needed seven stage models. — [Devblog 34, 14 Nov 2014](https://rust.facepunch.com/news/devblog-34)
- Building 3.0 (Dec 2017) made roofs occupy wall sockets so they can no longer be used as honeycomb layers, fixed several wall-stacking exploits, added half-height foundations and walls, and limited each building to one tool cupboard. — [Devblog 189](https://rust.facepunch.com/news/devblog-189)

**Island Architect [first-hand, interview]**
- Free placement easily leaves awkward spaces and gaps; a regular grid fills space but is rigid. His grid changes as you build so curved buildings, stairs and walls align; the price is that buildings and walls cannot stack inside each other. — [80.lv, 16 Jun 2025](https://80.lv/articles/island-architect-townscaper-inspired-city-builder-on-custom-engine)

**Seen only in search summaries [third-party unless noted]**
- An indie survival prototype first used one shared list of snap positions and found each structure type needs its own (pillars should snap to floor corners, not edges), then tagged each position with the structure it belongs to. [page returned HTTP 403] — [Vercidium on Patreon](https://www.patreon.com/vercidium/posts/huge-post-new-98247640)
- ARK: Survival Ascended revised snap points and Once Human 3.0.2 added cycling through compatible connection points during placement. — [GameRant](https://gamerant.com/ark-survival-ascended-base-building-tips/); [Topuplist](https://topuplist.com/blogs/detail/once-human-version-3-0-2-territory-snap-point-build-guide)
- Enshrouded previews describe voxel building with blueprint shapes and no structural-integrity limits. — [TechRaptor](https://techraptor.net/gaming/features/enshrouded-mixes-great-gameplay-into-epic-adventure)

### Inferences
- Three games independently converged on the same repair: when several snap points are valid, do not guess; let the author pick (Valheim's cycle, Once Human's cycle, Vercidium's typed points). In a text-first tool the equivalent is that an ambiguous join is an error or an explicit choice in the text, never a silent nearest-point pick.
- Satisfactory's hard and soft clearance is a clean two-class collision contract: pieces that must not overlap and pieces that may. It was introduced because one class for everything was too strict.
- No Man's Sky's position-dependent variants (foundation, middle, top) and Rust's roofs-occupy-wall-sockets rule both encode vertical role in the contract. Role by storey belongs in the socket type and should not be inferred from height alone.
- Tiny Glade shows the cost profile of having no fixed pieces: each new dimension (terrain, elevation, stairs) forced a rewrite of an existing generator and several discarded prototypes.
- Terrain appears in these accounts only as a source of trouble (clipping under foundations, arches breaking on slopes, slabs omitted). None describes a piece contract for slopes beyond stilts, supports and raising the base.

### Gaps
- No developer account of snapping or sockets was found for Enshrouded, Fortnite, Fallout 4's workshop, Cities: Skylines II or Manor Lords. Searches returned previews, player complaints and dev diaries on lot sizes only. The one relevant Manor Lords item, a developer note that the world is coded in 2D to simplify collisions, was seen only in a search summary. — [GOG forum copy of the April 2025 developer update](https://www.gog.com/forum/manor_lords/update_from_the_developer_april_2025)
- Valheim's structural-integrity rules are described only in player guides.
- I found no recorded talk by Opara or Stachowiak on Tiny Glade's building generation that could be read; the Steam dev diaries were read through Steam's public news feed because the announcement pages did not render.

## 6. Edits against regeneration: what was lost and which designs survived

### Takeaway
Three designs are reported to survive: store the author's input and regenerate everything else from it; store edits in world space or by local context instead of by index; and, when re-solving, bias the solver toward the previous result. Each has a stated breaking point.

### Cited Findings
- **Edits stored in world space.** Embark's viewport edits are saved as simple geometry inside parameters on the HDA, live in world space and carry any attributes needed, which is what keeps them stable when the building geometry updates. The stated condition: as long as the feature remains in the same location, the edit keeps applying. The post does not say what happens when the feature moves. — [SideFX](https://www.sidefx.com/community/making-the-procedural-buildings-of-the-finals-using-houdini/) [first-hand]
- **Input is the saved state.** In Townscaper, clicks change hidden solidity and colour layers and those act as constraints; the generator never edits visible tiles directly, and tile choice follows a fixed priority list so results are deterministic. — [Editable WFC, Boris the Brave, Apr 2022](https://www.boristhebrave.com/2022/04/25/editable-wfc/) [third-party analysis; he notes he does not fully understand some Townscaper behaviours]
- **Re-solve toward the previous result.** For editable WFC in general, one user edit can force other tiles to change, and plain regeneration returns a level with nothing in common with the previous one. His method forces the edited cell, re-picks only cells whose previous tile is no longer possible, chooses the tile most similar to the old one, and stops early by copying the rest. Drawbacks he states: it needs a tile similarity metric that may not exist, it is complex, early stopping may be unsafe with exotic constraints, and quality and performance leave something to be desired. It is often impossible to know in advance which cells will change. — [same](https://www.boristhebrave.com/2022/04/25/editable-wfc/) [first-hand]
- **Regeneration reaching the player.** Kleineberg's backtracking occasionally regenerated the cell the player was standing in. — [marian42.de](https://marian42.de/article/wfc/) [first-hand]
- **Local recomputation.** Tiny Glade recomputes only walls in regions touched by a path or terrain edit; generated supports "re-adapt" when elevated pieces move; windows re-adapt when the roof changes. After the stairs update the game saves after every operation, writing only the relevant updates. — [Dev diary, 14 Mar 2024](https://steamcommunity.com/games/2198150/announcements/detail/5970156536060713335); [Stairs update](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/2198150/announcements/detail/561377319446381084) [first-hand]
- **Unlinking.** Unreal's ClearPCGLink moves generated resources under a new actor and unlinks them from the component, so the output stops regenerating and becomes ordinary editable content. — [Epic API docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/API/Plugins/PCG/UPCGComponent/ClearPCGLink) [search summary; docs page not opened]
- **Cascading rebuilds.** In City Sample PCG, modifying a lower-numbered graph automatically rebuilds every later graph. — [Epic docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/city-sample-pcg-for-unreal-engine?lang=en-US) [docs]
- **Tool changes that need a parameter tweak mean a re-export.** In Shanty Town, parameters were editable in Unity but any change inside Houdini required re-exporting the asset. — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/procedural-environment-for-unity-with-houdini) [first-hand]
- **CityEngine local edits.** The paper by Esri's team proposes locating each edit by its local spatial context ("meta-locators"), computed independently per attribute, to make edits robust against changes in the procedural system. A CityEngine changelog entry says a compiler fix potentially changes existing local edits. [search summaries of the paper's abstract and of the changelog; neither opened] — [Local Editing of Procedural Models, CGF 2019](https://diglib.eg.org/items/3b6d6f2a-6b98-4f68-9b54-03033d917da2/full); [CityEngine CGA changelog](https://doc.arcgis.com/en/cityengine/2023.1/cga/cga-changelog.htm)
- **Unreal 5.8 manual edits.** A third-party blog says UE 5.8 PCG added manual edits that persist across regeneration, notes procedural instances have no stable identity by default, and advises scepticism about every case. [search summary; secondary; not verified against release notes] — [StraySpark blog](https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/ue5-8-pcg-manual-artist-edits)

### Inferences
- World-space anchoring (Embark) and index anchoring fail in opposite cases: world-space survives reordering and re-solving but not a moved building; indices survive moves but not a changed piece count. Anchoring an override to a named element in the source text plus a position within it (storey, bay, side) would survive both, at the cost of requiring stable names.
- Townscaper's and Tiny Glade's approach, where only the author's input is stored and all output is derived, avoids the lost-edit problem by not having output edits at all. It works when every wanted result can be expressed as input. The Embark case shows artists still ask for per-element overrides once buildings are large.
- "Unlink and freeze" is the escape hatch every pipeline keeps. It ends the lost-edit problem by ending regeneration, so a design should make clear which state a building is in.
- Determinism matters as much as ids: Townscaper's fixed priority order is what makes the same input give the same output. Random tie-breaking anywhere in assembly turns every re-run into a potential lost edit.

### Gaps
- I found no first-hand account from a studio describing hand changes being wiped by a generator re-run in production. The evidence here is the designs built to prevent it.
- CityEngine's own page on managing local edits was not found; the overview page opened contains nothing on persistence. — [CityEngine local edits overview](https://doc.arcgis.com/en/cityengine/2021.1/help/help-local-edits-overview.htm)
- No source was found on how Houdini Engine recooks treat per-instance overrides.

## 7. Teams that built a generator and dropped it, or the reverse

### Takeaway
Clear cases of a building generator being dropped are few and brief. What the sources show more often is a retreat in scope: from generator to toolset, from generating buildings to generating only the shell, from random to authored with procedural fill.

### Cited Findings
- **Tried and rejected.** CD Projekt Red tested procedural tools for Night City and was not satisfied; the environment art director says they did not come close to the quality and complexity wanted. The only procedural help was a spline road tool whose output artists then adjusted by hand. — [80.lv interview with Kacper Niepokólczycki](https://80.lv/articles/interview-how-cyberpunk-2077-s-night-city-was-built-almost-entirely-by-hand/) [first-hand, interview]
- **Generator narrowed to a toolset.** Embark concluded the Building Creator had to be building blocks and not a rigid generator, and on ARC Raiders used it only for base meshes with details hand-placed as instances. — [SideFX](https://www.sidefx.com/community/making-the-procedural-buildings-of-the-finals-using-houdini/) [first-hand]
- **Random first, control added after.** Shanty Town's author developed random mode heavily and then had to add a manual mode because the level designer needed control. — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/procedural-environment-for-unity-with-houdini) [first-hand]
- **Hand-designed frame, generated fill.** Shadows of Doubt's developer designs floorplans by hand in a dedicated tool and generates only the room subdivision inside each address. — [DevBlog 13](https://colepowered.com/?p=34642) [first-hand]
- **Prototype shelved.** Stålberg shelved Brick Block over repetition and returned to the idea years later with a different grid and tile scheme (Townscaper). For Bad North he considered and rejected pre-curated seeds in favour of a navigability heuristic. — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/how-townscaper-works-a-story-four-games-in-the-making) [first-hand, interview]
- **Technique judged unfit.** Kleineberg concluded WFC is unsuitable for commercial infinite-world games because of late-discovered errors and backtracking. — [marian42.de](https://marian42.de/article/wfc/) [first-hand]
- **Off-the-shelf node to custom tool.** The Project ZERO developer, unable to fix corner overlaps and gaps in the Labs node, was considering a custom generator while calling it incredibly time consuming; no decision is recorded. — [itch.io devlog](https://tubbytobe.itch.io/project-zero/devlog/877134/devlog-2-290125) [first-hand, indie]
- **Hand assembly to generator.** Embark's stated reason for building the tool was that earlier projects rebuilt destruction setups by hand after every building change. — [SideFX](https://www.sidefx.com/community/making-the-procedural-buildings-of-the-finals-using-houdini/) [first-hand]. EXE Collective built theirs because the city was too large for three people to model. — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/procedural-buildings-in-ue4-logic-behind-the-tool) [first-hand]
- **Seen only in search summaries.** An 80.lv interview with a technical production lead describes procedural tools giving a first high-level pass that artists then individualise, says some find the generated meshes or levels lacking individuality, and says that at small indie scale it is often easier and cheaper to make such assets by hand. — [80.lv: The art of technical production in games](https://80.lv/articles/the-art-of-technical-production-in-games) [search summary; not opened]. Introversion put Subversion on hold in 2011; Chris Delay's stated reason was too long spent on technology and not on the core game. It was a heist game with procedural content, not a building generator as such. — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/au/introversions-chris-delay-on-shifting-from-subversion-to-prison-architect-i-wanted-to-build-alcatraz) [search summary; not opened]

### Inferences
- The stated reasons for dropping or narrowing are quality ceiling (CDPR), style range (Embark), loss of designer control (Shanty Town) and unexplained failure (Kleineberg, Project ZERO). None cites cost of the generator itself.
- The reasons for adopting one are all about change, not first construction: rebuilding after every edit (Embark) or sheer count (EXE Collective). This matches the earlier report's finding that the cost of kits is in change.
- The arrangement that persists across studio and indie accounts is a hand-authored frame with generated fill, with a way to take any generated part back into hand control.

### Gaps
- No detailed postmortem of a studio abandoning a working building generator for hand assembly was found. CDPR's statement is two sentences and does not say which tools were tested or what specifically fell short.
- No account was found of the reverse at a studio (a hand-assembled city later converted to a generator) beyond the motivations quoted above.
