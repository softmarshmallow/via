# 0004 — How do lakes form? Do they last?

**Status: partially open (ledger).**

**Q:** How does the lake get formed — do we have "a lake", or does that
part inherently become a lake?

**A:** The latter, strictly. A lake is a *diagnosis*, never a placed
object: wherever the evolving surface encloses a depression whose maximum
ponding depth is ≥ 0.5 m, water stands at the spill elevation (ADR 0004).
Today depressions have one source — the uplift field's geometry (tier-2
forcing). From there everything is emergent: rivers grade to the lake
surface, deltas prograde into it, and the basin fills toward becoming a
lacustrine plain.

**Lakes are mortal, and the model reproduces that.** Real lakes are
geologically young features; landscapes fill them. Our 30 Myr research
runs end lake-free; the 5 Myr island held one under D8 and mostly filled
it under M3.5's more efficient sediment delivery. Lake persistence is
seed- and age-dependent — for game use this is a *selection* criterion,
not something to force.

Three kinds of "lake", three different homes:

1. **Topographic lakes** (depressions, dammed basins) — must live inside
   the terrain core because they set erosion's base level. **Done (M3).**
2. **Terminal/evaporative lakes** (endorheic basins, salt flats) — today
   every lake spills to the sea because routed water never evaporates.
   Needs an evaporation-aware lake water budget in the core.
   **Open (ledger).**
3. **Oxbow lakes** — floodplain features from meander cutoffs; belong in
   a downstream river-corridor stage, not the core. **Open (ledger)** —
   see FAQ 0005.

Also absent as depression *sources*: glacial scour, fault grabens,
calderas, landslide dams. Grabens arrive naturally with M4 structure.
